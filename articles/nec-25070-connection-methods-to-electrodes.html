NEC 250.70 — Methods of Grounding and Bonding Conductor Connection to Electrodes

The last section of Article 250 Part III — and the one that finishes the on-disk Part III build. The previous articles picked the electrodes (250.50/250.52/250.53), sized the GEC (250.66 table + caps), and ran it (250.64). 250.68 said where the GEC lands on the electrode. 250.70 says how it physically lands: exothermic welding, listed lugs, listed pressure connectors, listed clamps, or other listed means — never solder — plus the ground-clamp listing rules, the one-conductor-per-clamp limit, and (in the 2017 edition) a four-methods hardware list that the 2023 reorganization removed.

The section, in full (2017)

250.70 Methods of Grounding and Bonding Conductor Connection to Electrodes. The grounding or bonding conductor shall be connected to the grounding electrode by exothermic welding, listed lugs, listed pressure connectors, listed clamps, or other listed means. Connections depending on solder shall not be used. Ground clamps shall be listed for the materials of the grounding electrode and the grounding electrode conductor and, where used on pipe, rod, or other buried electrodes, shall also be listed for direct soil burial or concrete encasement. Not more than one conductor shall be connected to the grounding electrode by a single clamp or fitting unless the clamp or fitting is listed for multiple conductors. One of the following methods shall be used: (1) A pipe fitting, pipe plug, or other approved device screwed into a pipe or pipe fitting (2) A listed bolted clamp of cast bronze or brass, or plain or malleable iron (3) For indoor communications purposes only, a listed sheet metal strap-type ground clamp having a rigid metal base that seats on the electrode and having a strap of such material and dimensions that it is not likely to stretch during or after installation (4) An equally substantial approved means Source: verbatim 2017 NEC (official NFPA text on disk, nec2017_full.txt). OCR line-wrap reflow normalized; the 2017 scan's "conduc-tors" hyphen split at a line break is rejoined to "conductors" (line-wrap artifact, disclosed). No other wording altered.
One sentence, three jobs. (1) Permitted methods — exothermic welding, listed lugs, listed pressure connectors, listed clamps, or other listed means, and the hard "no solder" rule. (2) Clamp listing — the clamp must be listed for both the electrode material and the GEC material, and (for buried electrodes) for direct soil burial or concrete encasement. (3) Capacity — one conductor per clamp/fitting unless the fitting is listed for multiple conductors. The 2017 four-methods list at the end is the part that changed the most between editions — next section.

Why this is the capstone of Part III

Part III (Grounding Electrode Systems) is a sequence, and 250.70 is the last step. The prior articles each own one link in the chain, and 250.70 closes it:

  • What the electrode system is: 250.50 required electrode + 250.52 types + the 250.53(C) bonding jumper — the 250.50 + 250.52 + 250.53 article →.
  • Size the GEC: Table 250.66 + the three caps (6 AWG to rods, 4 AWG to the Ufer, ring-size to a ground ring) — the 250.54/58/60/62/66/68 article → (the cap math lives there; this article shows where the resulting GEC physically lands).
  • Run the GEC: 250.64 (no-splice, protection) + 250.104 (bond pipes/structural metal) — the 250.64 + 250.104 article →.
  • Land it on the electrode (location): 250.68 — the 5-ft water-pipe rule, hold-down bolts, the rebar stub up, accessibility — the 250.54/58/60/62/66/68 article →.
  • Land it on the electrode (method): 250.70 — this article. The conductor 250.66 sized and 250.68 positioned is actually attached here, by a listed means, with no solder.

Two adjacent sections that 250.70 leans on and that do not yet have their own page: 250.8 (Connection of Grounding and Bonding Equipment — the general permitted-methods list for EGCs, GECs, and bonding jumpers) and 250.10 (Protection of Ground Clamps and Fittings). Both are quoted below; 250.8 is where the "what counts as a listed connection" vocabulary comes from, and 250.10 is the physical-damage protection rule for the clamps 250.70 requires.

250.8 and 250.10 — the two sections 250.70 leans on

250.70 is electrode-specific. The general connection rules live in 250.8, and 250.70's clamps are the ones 250.10 protects. Both are word-identical 2017 → 2023 (verified against the on-disk 2023 CSV).

250.8 Connection of Grounding and Bonding Equipment. (A) Permitted Methods. Equipment grounding conductors, grounding electrode conductors, and bonding jumpers shall be connected by one or more of the following means: (1) Listed pressure connectors (2) Terminal bars (3) Pressure connectors listed as grounding and bonding equipment (4) Exothermic welding process (5) Machine screw-type fasteners that engage not less than two threads or are secured with a nut (6) Thread-forming machine screws that engage not less than two threads in the enclosure (7) Connections that are part of a listed assembly (8) Other listed means (B) Methods Not Permitted. Connection devices or fittings that depend solely on solder shall not be used. Source: verbatim 2017 NEC on disk (nec2017_full.txt); 2023 CSV rows 250.8(A)/(B) word-identical.
250.10 Protection of Ground Clamps and Fittings. Ground clamps or other fittings exposed to physical damage shall be enclosed in metal, wood, or equivalent protective covering. Source: verbatim 2017 NEC on disk (nec2017_full.txt); 2023 CSV row 250.10 word-identical.
How they relate. 250.8 lists the general permitted connection means for any grounding/bonding conductor (terminal bars, machine screws that engage ≥2 threads, listed assemblies, …). 250.70 then narrows it to the electrode connection — and adds the electrode-specific clamp-listing and one-conductor-per-clamp rules that 250.8 does not carry. 250.10 is the maintenance-side companion: whatever clamp 250.70 mandates, if it's exposed to physical damage, it needs a protective covering. Solder is banned in both 250.8(B) and 250.70 — the electrode connection is no place to rely on a eutectic joint.

Edition history: 2017 → 2020 → 2023

250.70 is one of the few Part III sections that genuinely changed structure between editions. The story is a 2023 reorganization — and it's a removal, not an addition.

2017 → 2020: unchanged (word-identical)

The 2020 text of 250.70 is word-identical to 2017 — same single flat paragraph, same "where used on pipe, rod, or other buried electrodes" phrasing, same four-methods list (1)–(4). Source: ELR sectionID 1005.0 "2020 Code Language" (fetched 2026-09-15). No 2017→2020 change to record.

2020 → 2023: reorganized into (A) General + (B), methods list removed

Per the 2023 NEC change summary (Mike Holt 2023 Code Change Summaries, Article 250): "250.70 … This section was reorganized into two first level subdivisions and editorially revised for clarity. An Informational Note providing helpful information was also added." Concretely, the on-disk 2023 text (2023 NEC CSV, rows 250.70(A)/250.70(B), cross-checked against the ELR 2023 "Code Language" block) shows exactly what that means:

250.70 Methods of Grounding and Bonding Conductor Connection to Electrodes. (A) General. The grounding or bonding conductor shall be connected to the grounding electrode by exothermic welding, listed lugs, listed pressure connectors, listed clamps, or other listed means. Connections depending on solder shall not be used. Ground clamps shall be listed for the materials of the grounding electrode and the grounding electrode conductor and, if used on pipe, rod, or other buried electrodes, shall also be listed for direct soil burial or concrete encasement. Not more than one conductor shall be connected to the grounding electrode by a single clamp or fitting unless the clamp or fitting is listed for multiple conductors. (B) For indoor communications purposes only, a listed sheet metal strap-type ground clamp having a rigid metal base that seats on the electrode and having a strap of such material and dimensions that it is not likely to stretch during or after installation shall be permitted. Informational Note: Listed ground clamps that are identified for direct burial are also suitable for concrete encasement. Source: verbatim 2023 NEC (on-disk 2023 CSV, rows 250.70(A)/250.70(B)), cross-checked against ELR sectionID 1607 "2023 Code Language" and the Mike Holt 2023 Code Change Summaries (p.17).
What changed — four deltas, all verified.
  1. Structure: the 2017/2020 single flat paragraph became two first-level subdivisions, (A) General + (B).
  2. The four-methods list (1)–(4) was removed. The 2017 items — (1) pipe fitting/pipe plug screwed into pipe, (2) listed bolted clamp of cast bronze or brass or plain or malleable iron, (3) the communications strap-type ground clamp, (4) an equally substantial approved means — are gone from 250.70 in 2023. Only the communications strap-type clamp (former item 3) survives, re-cast as the stand-alone (B) sentence.
  3. Editorial: "where used on pipe, rod, or other buried electrodes" (2017/2020) → "if used on pipe, rod, or other buried electrodes" (2023 (A)).
  4. New Informational Note added to (B): "Listed ground clamps that are identified for direct burial are also suitable for concrete encasement."

What did NOT change: the (A) opening — "exothermic welding, listed lugs, listed pressure connectors, listed clamps, or other listed means" — is word-identical 2017→2023, as is the no-solder sentence and the one-conductor-per-clamp sentence. So the core rule (the methods + the no-solder + the clamp listing + the one-conductor limit) is stable across all three editions; only the enumerated hardware list and the subdivision structure moved.

Read the removal carefully — the hardware still works, the code just stopped enumerating it. A pipe plug on a water pipe (2017 item 1) and a listed bolted clamp on a rod (2017 item 2) are not banned in 2023 — they are simply no longer spelled out in 250.70's numbered list. They now ride the (A) General language: a pipe plug is a "listed lug / listed pressure connector / other listed means," and a bolted clamp is a "listed clamp." The 2023 text is narrower in wording, not in permit. If your adopted edition is 2017 or 2020, the four-methods list is the explicit citation; if it is 2023, cite (A) General (and (B) for the communications clamp). Verify against the adopted edition.

The Ufer stub-up is the textbook case for exothermic welding: you're bonding a 4 AWG GEC to a chunk of rebar, and welding is the cleanest "effective grounding path" (250.68(B)) you can make. Note the 2023 Informational Note's payoff here — a Ufer is concrete-encased, and the Note says a clamp identified for direct burial is also suitable for concrete encasement, so one listing covers the buried and concrete-encased cases.

The "never solder" rule, in context

Both 250.8(B) and 250.70 ban connections that depend solely on solder, and both have carried that ban across all three editions. The reason is practical: a solder joint at an electrode is a ground-fault-current path, not a normal current path. On a fault, the heat of the fault current can melt a eutectic joint exactly when you need the path to hold. Exothermic welding (a metallurgical bond, not a filler-metal joint), listed lugs, and listed pressure connectors survive that event; a solder fillet is a gamble. This is why 250.70's opening leads with "exothermic welding" and the 2023 (B) Informational Note exists to tell you a direct-burial-listed clamp also covers concrete — the code is telling you which listed means actually hold up in the ground.
