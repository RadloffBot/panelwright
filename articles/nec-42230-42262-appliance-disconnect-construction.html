Each disconnecting means shall simultaneously disconnect all ungrounded conductors that it controls. NEC 2017 (NFPA 70), Article 422 Part III — verbatim on-disk official scan. 422.33 Disconnection of Cord-and-Plug-Connected or Attachment Fitting—Connected Appliances. (A) Separable Connector or an Attachment Plug (or Attachment Fitting) and Receptacle. 422.34 Unit Switch(es) as Disconnecting Means. A unit switch(es) with a marked-off position that is a part of an appliance and disconnects all ungrounded conductors shall be permitted as the disconnecting means required by this article where other means for disconnection are provided in occupancies specified in 422.34(A) through (D). (A) Multifamily Dwellings. In multifamily dwellings, the other disconnecting means shall be within the dwelling unit, or on the same floor as the dwelling unit in which the appliance is installed, and shall be permitted to control lamps and other appliances. (B) Two-Family Dwellings. In two-family dwellings, the other disconnecting means shall be permitted either inside or outside of the dwelling unit in which the appliance is installed. In this case, an individual switch or circuit breaker for the dwelling unit shall be permitted and shall also be permitted to control lamps and other appliances. (C) One-Family Dwellings. In one-family dwellings, the service disconnecting means shall be permitted to be the other disconnecting means. (D) Other Occupancies. In other occupancies, the branch-circuit switch or circuit breaker, where readily accessible for servicing of the appliance, shall be permitted as the other disconnecting means. The 2017→2023 edition story — Part III (8 deltas, machine-verified)

422.31 gets a routing lead (NEW in 2023). The 2023 edition opens 422.31 with a sentence the 2017 edition does not have: "For appliances that do not have a disconnecting means in accordance with 422.33 or 422.34, a disconnecting means shall be provided in accordance with 422.31(A), (B), or (C)." It is explicit signposting: the plug (422.33) and the unit switch (422.34) are checked FIRST, and 422.31 is the route for everything else.
422.31(A)/(B): the lockable clause is reworded. 2017: "…is within sight from the appliance or is lockable in accordance with 110.25." 2023 (both (A) and (B)): "…is within sight from the appliance or be capable of being locked in the open position in compliance with 110.25." Same 110.25 anchor, same two routes — the 2023 wording is the phrase the 422.31(C) rule already used in 2017, so the section now speaks with one voice. (Machine-verified: "is lockable in accordance with 110.25" is absent from every in-scope 2023 row.)
422.31(B) Informational Note reworded. 2017: "For appliances employing unit switches, see 422.34." → 2023: "See 422.34 for appliances employing unit switches." Pure editorial — the pointer is the same.
422.31(C) Exception: the "more than 4 hp" gate is REMOVED (the substantive Part III delta). 2017: "If an appliance of more than 4 hp is provided with a unit switch that complies with 422.34(A), (B), (C), or (D), the switch or circuit breaker serving as the other disconnecting means shall be permitted to be out of sight from the appliance." 2023: "If an appliance is provided with a unit switch that complies with 422.34(A), (B), or (C), …" — two changes in one sentence: (1) the 4-hp minimum is gone, so a ½ hp motor-operated appliance with a compliant unit switch may now have the other disconnecting means out of sight (in 2017 it had to be in sight or lockable-open); (2) the "(D)" (other-occupancies) route is dropped from the exception list. The 430.109/430.110 compliance requirement in the (C) lead is unchanged in both editions.
422.33(B): "shall meet the intent of 422.33(A)" → "shall be permitted" (Mike Holt-documented). 2017: a rear-base range plug/receptacle "accessible from the front by removal of a drawer, shall meet the intent of 422.33(A)." 2023: the same sentence ends "…shall be permitted." The Mike Holt 2023 change summary (on disk) documents exactly this: "The rule in (B) clarifies that a cord-and-plug connection for a range is considered accessible by the removal of the drawer at the front of the range." The practical rule is unchanged — it is the accessibility-by-drawer-removal that was always the point — but 2023 states it as a permitted method instead of an intent-matching argument.

EX1 — the 422.33(C) receptacle floor: a 50 A range on a 40 A receptacle

A 12,000 W / 240 V household electric range is cord-and-plug connected, so the accessible plug + receptacle IS the disconnecting means (422.33(A)). 422.33(C) then sets the floor: the receptacle rating must not be less than the rating of any appliance connected to it.

EX3 — the 422.31(A)/(B) boundary: 300 VA at 115 V

422.31 routes permanently connected appliances by the 300 VA / 1/8 hp boundary. At 115 V (a typical small-appliance circuit voltage) the boundary lands at 300 ÷ 115 = 2.61 A:

The two rows land on the same 15 A OCPD (both round up to the 240.6 floor) — the boundary changes the routing language (overcurrent device vs switch/breaker, and which of 422.31(A)/(B) you cite on the panel schedule), not the device. That is the point of the 300 VA threshold: small permanently connected appliances are allowed to live on their breaker alone, so long as it is in sight or lockable.

Where 422.30–422.62 fit

  • The branch-circuit foundation this article extends: the NEC 422.10 + 422.11 + 422.12 + 422.13 appliance branch circuit (article 56) — 422.10/422.11 size the circuit and OCPD; 422.13 makes the water heater a 125% continuous load (EX5's 23.44 A / 25 A / 12 AWG package is that article's EX1 re-run); this article is what happens AFTER the circuit is sized — the disconnect, the construction, the marking.
  • The Part II installation neighbor: the NEC 422.16 + 422.18 + 422.22 flexible-cords / ceiling-fan-support article (58) — 422.16's cord-and-plug conditions feed directly into 422.33 (the cord's plug IS a candidate disconnecting means); its documented 422.15/422.43 deletions frame this article's 422.46/422.50 deletions.
  • The motor-side rules 422.31(C) hangs on: the NEC 430.101–430.113 disconnecting means (article 42) — 422.31(C)'s "comply with 430.109 and 430.110" is a direct hand-off, and EX4's 430.110(C)(1) sum (4.0 + 1.09 = 5.09 A → 15 A) is that article's combined-loads rule applied to an appliance.
  • The single-motor circuit this appliance would sit on: the NEC 430.22 + 430.52 single-motor branch circuit (article 38) — the 125% FLC conductor rule and Table 430.52 OCPD values that a motor-operated appliance's branch circuit follows.
  • The OCPD catalog and the conductor table behind every rating above: the NEC 240.6 standard ampere ratings (the 15 A floor in EX3/EX4, the 45 A standard size in EX2, the 25 A step in EX5) and the Table 310.16 allowable ampacities (the 8 AWG / 12 AWG picks).
