NEC 450.21–450.28 — Transformer Installation by Type (Article 450 Part II)

Articles 51 and 52 covered the protection core (450.3 / 450.4 / 450.6) and the general installation core (450.8–450.14) of Article 450. This article is Part II — "Specific Provisions Applicable to Different Types of Transformers" — the rules that decide where a transformer may physically live, keyed on its insulation type: 450.21 dry-type indoors (the 112½ kVA transformer-room boundary + the Class 155 exceptions), 450.22 dry-type outdoors (weatherproof enclosure + the 12-in combustible-material rule), 450.23 less-flammable liquids (the 300 °C fire point; the 35,000 V indoor cap), 450.24 nonflammable fluids (indoors or outdoors; vault over 35,000 V), 450.25 askarel (the 25 kVA vent / 35,000 V vault pair), 450.26 oil-insulated indoors (the vault rule + all six Exceptions), 450.27 oil-insulated outdoors (the four fire safeguards), and 450.28 modification marking. Part III — the vault construction itself (450.41–450.48) — is the next article.

Part II. Specific Provisions Applicable to Different Types of Transformers 450.21 Dry-Type Transformers Installed Indoors. (A) Not Over 112½ kVA. Dry-type transformers installed indoors and rated 112½ kVA or less shall have a separation of at least 300 mm (12 in.) from combustible material unless separated from the combustible material by a fire-resistant, heat-insulated barrier. Exception: This rule shall not apply to transformers rated for 1000 volts, nominal, or less that are completely enclosed, except for ventilating openings. (B) Over 112½ kVA. Individual dry-type transformers of more than 112½ kVA rating shall be installed in a transformer room of fire-resistant construction. Unless specified otherwise in this article, the term fire resistant means a construction having a minimum fire rating of 1 hour. Exception No. 1: Transformers with Class 155 or higher insulation systems and separated from combustible material by a fire-resistant, heat-insulating barrier or by not less than 1.83 m (6 ft) horizontally and 3.7 m (12 ft) vertically, Exception No. 2: Transformers with Class 155 or higher insulation systems and completely enclosed except for ventilating openings. Informational Note: See ANSI/ASTM E119-15, Method for Fire Tests of Building Construction and Materials. (C) Over 35,000 Volts. Dry-type transformers rated over 35,000 volts shall be installed in a vault complying with Part III of this article. 450.22 Dry-Type Transformers Installed Outdoors. Dry-type transformers installed outdoors shall have a weatherproof enclosure. Transformers exceeding 112½ kVA shall not be located within 300 mm (12 in.) of combustible materials of buildings unless the transformer has Class 155 insulation systems or higher and is completely enclosed except for ventilating openings. 450.23 Less-Flammable Liquid-Insulated Transformers. Transformers insulated with listed less-flammable liquids that have a fire point of not less than 300°C shall be permitted to be installed in accordance with 450.23(A) or 450.23(B). (A) Indoor Installations. Indoor installations shall be permitted in accordance with one of the following: (1) In Type I or Type II buildings, in areas where all of the following requirements are met: The transformer is rated 35,000 volts or less. No combustible materials are stored. A liquid confinement area is provided. The installation complies with all the restrictions provided for in the listing of the liquid. Informational Note: Such restrictions may include, but are not limited to: maximum pressure of the tank, use of a pressure relief valve, appropriate fuse types and proper sizing of overcurrent protection. (2) With an automatic fire extinguishing system and a liquid confinement area, provided the transformer is rated 35,000 volts or less (3) In accordance with 450.26 (B) Outdoor Installations. Less-flammable liquid-filled transformers shall be permitted to be installed outdoors, attached to, adjacent to, or on the roof of buildings, where installed in accordance with (1) or (2). (1) For Type I and Type II buildings, the installation shall comply with all the restrictions provided for in the listing of the liquid. Informational Note No. 1: Installations adjacent to combustible material, fire escapes, or door and window openings may require additional safeguards such as those listed in 450.27. Informational Note No. 2: Such restrictions may include, but are not limited to: maximum pressure of the tank, use of a pressure relief valve, appropriate fuse types, and proper sizing of overcurrent protection. (2) In accordance with 450.27. Informational Note No. 1: As used in this section, Type I and Type II buildings refers to Type I and Type II building construction as defined in NFPA 220-2015, Standard on Types of Building Construction. Combustible materials refers to those materials not classified as noncombustible or limited-combustible as defined in NFPA 220-2015, Standard on Types of Building Construction. Informational Note No. 2: See definition of Listed in Article 100. 450.24 Nonflammable Fluid-Insulated Transformers. Transformers insulated with a dielectric fluid identified as nonflammable shall be permitted to be installed indoors or outdoors. Such transformers installed indoors and rated over 35,000 volts shall be installed in a vault. Such transformers installed indoors shall be furnished with a liquid confinement area and a pressure-relief vent. The transformers shall be furnished with a means for absorbing any gases generated by arcing inside the tank, or the pressure-relief vent shall be connected to a chimney or flue that will carry such gases to an environmentally safe area. Informational Note: Safety may be increased if fire hazard analyses are performed for such transformer installations. For the purposes of this section, a nonflammable dielectric fluid is one that does not have a flash point or fire point and is not flammable in air. 450.25 Askarel-Insulated Transformers Installed Indoors. Askarel-insulated transformers installed indoors and rated over 25 kVA shall be furnished with a pressure-relief vent. Where installed in a poorly ventilated place, they shall be furnished with a means for absorbing any gases generated by arcing inside the case, or the pressure-relief vent shall be connected to a chimney or flue that carries such gases outside the building. Askarel-insulated transformers rated over 35,000 volts shall be installed in a vault. 450.26 Oil-Insulated Transformers Installed Indoors. Oil-insulated transformers installed indoors shall be installed in a vault constructed as specified in Part III of this article. Exception No. 1: Where the total capacity does not exceed 112½ kVA, the vault specified in Part III of this article shall be permitted to be constructed of reinforced concrete that is not less than 100 mm (4 in.) thick. Exception No. 2: Where the nominal voltage does not exceed 1000, a vault shall not be required if suitable arrangements are made to prevent a transformer oil fire from igniting other materials and the total capacity in one location does not exceed 10 kVA in a section of the building classified as combustible or 75 kVA where the surrounding structure is classified as fire-resistant construction. Exception No. 3: Electric furnace transformers that have a total rating not exceeding 75 kVA shall be permitted to be installed without a vault in a building or room of fire-resistant construction, provided suitable arrangements are made to prevent a transformer oil fire from spreading to other combustible material. Exception No. 4: A transformer that has a total rating not exceeding 75 kVA and a supply voltage of 1000 volts or less that is an integral part of charged-particle-accelerating equipment shall be permitted to be installed without a vault in a building or room of noncombustible or fire-resistant construction, provided suitable arrangements are made to prevent a transformer oil fire from spreading to other combustible material. Exception No. 5: Transformers shall be permitted to be installed in a detached building that does not comply with Part III of this article if neither the building nor its contents present a fire hazard to any other building or property, and if the building is used only in supplying electric service and the interior is accessible only to qualified persons. Exception No. 6: Oil-insulated transformers shall be permitted to be used without a vault in portable and mobile surface mining equipment (such as electric excavators) if each of the following conditions is met: (1) Provision is made for draining leaking fluid to the ground. (2) Safe egress is provided for personnel. (3) A minimum 6-mm (1/4-in.) steel barrier is provided for personnel protection. 450.27 Oil-Insulated Transformers Installed Outdoors. Combustible material, combustible buildings, and parts of buildings, fire escapes, and door and window openings shall be safeguarded from fires originating in oil-insulated transformers installed on roofs, attached to or adjacent to a building or combustible material. In cases where the transformer installation presents a fire hazard, one or more of the following safeguards shall be applied according to the degree of hazard involved: (1) Space separations (2) Fire-resistant barriers (3) Automatic fire suppression systems (4) Enclosures that confine the oil of a ruptured transformer tank Oil enclosures shall be permitted to consist of fire-resistant dikes, curbed areas or basins, or trenches filled with coarse, crushed stone. Oil enclosures shall be provided with trapped drains where the exposure and the quantity of oil involved are such that removal of oil is important. Informational Note: For additional information on transformers installed on poles or structures or under ground, see ANSI C2-2007, National Electrical Safety Code. 450.28 Modification of Transformers. When modifications are made to a transformer in an existing installation that change the type of the transformer with respect to Part II of this article, such transformer shall be marked to show the type of insulating liquid installed, and the modified transformer installation shall comply with the applicable requirements for that type of transformer. The 112½ kVA figure appears three times (450.21(A)/(B) indoors, 450.22 outdoors, and 450.26 Ex 1's concrete vault) — it is the recurring "small transformer" boundary of Part II. The 35,000 V figure is the recurring medium-voltage boundary (dry-type → Part III vault; less-flammable indoor routes cap; askarel → vault). The 75 kVA figure is the recurring oil-indoors exception boundary (fire-resistant no-vault; furnace; accelerator).

450.21 — Dry-type indoors: the 112½ kVA boundary

This is the section people hit first when a dry-type unit lands inside a building:

  • (A) Not over 112½ kVA. A separation of at least 300 mm (12 in.) from combustible material — or a fire-resistant, heat-insulated barrier in place of the distance. The Exception is the one that puts pad-mount-style dry-type units against walls legally: at 1000 V nominal or less and completely enclosed except for ventilating openings, even the 12 inches is not required.
  • (B) Over 112½ kVA. A transformer room of fire-resistant construction — 1-hour minimum fire rating (2023 fuses the rating into the rule; 2017 stated it as a term definition). Exception No. 1: Class 155 or higher insulation systems with a fire-resistant, heat-insulating barrier or separation of not less than 1.83 m (6 ft) horizontally and 3.7 m (12 ft) vertically. Exception No. 2: Class 155 or higher, completely enclosed except for ventilating openings. This is why the temperature class on the nameplate (450.11(A)(8) — Article 52) is a Part II input: a Class 155 dry-type unit at 150 kVA can sit in a normal room; a Class 120 unit at 150 kVA needs the 1-hour room.
  • (C) Over 35,000 V. Part III vault — regardless of the size or insulation class. The medium-voltage boundary.

450.22 — Dry-type outdoors

Two sentences: weatherproof enclosure, and for units exceeding 112½ kVA, no location within 300 mm (12 in.) of combustible materials of buildings — unless Class 155 insulation or higher + completely enclosed except for ventilating openings. Note the asymmetry with 450.21: outdoors there is no barrier alternative to the 12 inches, and the insulation-class escape is Class 155-or-higher (450.21(B)'s barrier-or-distance Exception has no outdoor counterpart).

450.23 — Less-flammable liquid (the 300 °C fire point)

The gateway sentence: the liquid must be listed with a fire point of not less than 300 °C. (Fire point, not flash point — the liquid is permitted to be less flammable than oil, not nonflammable like 450.24's fluids.)

  • (A) Indoor. Three routes: (1) Type I or Type II building where all of — 35,000 V or less, no combustible materials stored, liquid confinement area provided, installation complies with the listing's restrictions (the IN enumerates them: maximum tank pressure, pressure relief valve, appropriate fuse types, proper OCPD sizing); (2) an automatic fire extinguishing system + liquid confinement area, 35,000 V or less; (3) in accordance with 450.26 (the oil-insulated vault route). The 35,000 V cap is the boundary: a 40 kV unit has only route (3) — a vault.
  • (B) Outdoor. Attached to, adjacent to, or on the roof of buildings — per (1) the listing's restrictions (Type I/II buildings) or (2) in accordance with 450.27 (the outdoor oil safeguards, by reference). The 2017 (B)(2) Informational Note No. 1 defines "Type I and Type II buildings" via NFPA 220-2015 (2023 re-cites NFPA 220-2021) and defines combustible materials as those not classified noncombustible or limited-combustible.

450.24 — Nonflammable fluid

The top of the fluid hierarchy: a dielectric fluid identified as nonflammable — the section's own definition: one that does not have a flash point or fire point and is not flammable in air. Permitted indoors or outdoors. The three indoor obligations: vault if over 35,000 V; a liquid confinement area + pressure-relief vent; and a means for absorbing gases generated by arcing inside the tank, or the pressure-relief vent connected to a chimney or flue carrying the gases to an environmentally safe area. The Informational Note suggests fire hazard analyses "may" increase safety — permissive, not mandatory.

450.25 — Askarel (PCB) — the legacy fluid

Askarel is the older PCB-based fluid (polychlorinated biphenyls) — still present in service on existing units, which is why the rule survives. Two boundaries: over 25 kVA indoors → pressure-relief vent (the word "over" matters: at exactly 25 kVA no vent is required); poorly ventilated place → gas absorption or the vent routed to a chimney/flue outside the building; over 35,000 V → vault. Word-identical 2017 → 2023.

450.26 — Oil-insulated indoors: the vault + six Exceptions

The default is the strictest in Part II: indoor oil-filled = Part III vault. The six Exceptions, in the order an inspector will ask about them:

  1. 112½ kVA or less → the vault may be reinforced concrete, not less than 100 mm (4 in.) thick, instead of full Part III construction (the 4-inch concrete vault — the cheapest legal indoor oil-filled option).
  2. 1000 V nominal or less + suitable arrangements to prevent an oil fire from igniting other materials → no vault up to 10 kVA in a section classified as combustible, or 75 kVA where the surrounding structure is fire-resistant. This is the exception that covers most small indoor pad-mount-adjacent oil units.
  3. Electric furnace transformers, total rating ≤ 75 kVA → no vault in a fire-resistant building/room, with suitable arrangements.
  4. Charged-particle-accelerating equipment (medical linear accelerators and the like): ≤ 75 kVA, supply 1000 V or less, integral to the equipment → no vault in noncombustible or fire-resistant construction, with suitable arrangements.
  5. Detached building that does not comply with Part III — if it presents no fire hazard to other buildings/property, is used only in supplying electric service, and the interior is accessible only to qualified persons (the "transformer shack" exception).
  6. Portable and mobile surface mining equipment (electric excavators): no vault if (1) draining leaking fluid to the ground, (2) safe egress, (3) a 6-mm (1/4-in.) steel barrier for personnel protection.

450.27 — Oil-insulated outdoors: the four fire safeguards

Outdoor oil-filled units (rooftop, pole-adjacent, pad-mounted next to the building) must safeguard combustible material, buildings, fire escapes, and door/window openings from transformer-origin fires. Where the installation presents a fire hazard, apply one or more — "according to the degree of hazard involved" is the AHJ's latitude clause:

  1. Space separations
  2. Fire-resistant barriers
  3. Automatic fire suppression systems
  4. Enclosures that confine the oil of a ruptured tank — fire-resistant dikes, curbed areas or basins, or trenches filled with coarse, crushed stone; trapped drains where the exposure and quantity of oil make removal important (a trapped drain keeps fire-extinguishing foam/chemicals in and keeps the oil from running off-site).

The Informational Note points to the National Electrical Safety Code for pole/structure/underground installations — 2017 cites ANSI C2-2007; 2023 cites ANSI/IEEE C2-2017.

450.28 — Modification of transformers

The catch-all for the retrofits: if a modification to an existing installation changes the transformer's type with respect to Part II (e.g., a fluid swap that moves the unit from oil to less-flammable, or a tank replacement that changes the insulation class), the transformer shall be marked to show the type of insulating liquid installed, and the modified installation shall comply with the applicable requirements for that type. The marking is the audit trail: a 450.11(A)(7) nameplate item (amount and kind of insulating liquid) that 450.28 enforces at modification time.

Net effect for an installation job. If you are placing a transformer, the requirements you satisfy are the same whether the jurisdiction is on 2017 or 2023: the 112½ kVA indoor/outdoor split, the 12-inch separations, the Class 155 escapes, the 1-hour transformer room, the 300 °C fire point, the 35,000 V medium-voltage boundaries, the 25 kVA askarel vent, the six 450.26 Exceptions, the four 450.27 safeguards, and the 450.28 modification marking. The pedagogical point of the boundary: stepping one kVA over 112½ doesn't change the conductors (169.15 A and 169.90 A both pick 2/0 AWG Cu at 175 A) — it changes the building. The 450.21(B) trigger is about fire exposure, not ampacity: the same 2/0 Cu feed serves both units, but the 113 kVA unit needs a 1-hour room unless it is Class 155.

EX2 — 450.26 Exception 2: the no-vault indoor oil boundaries (≤ 1000 V), 480 V

The two boundaries are not interchangeable: a 25 kVA indoor oil unit at 480 V cannot use Exception 2 in a combustible section (10 kVA max) — but it can use Exception 1: a 4-inch reinforced-concrete vault instead of the full Part III vault. The 112½ kVA concrete-vault route and the 75 kVA fire-resistant no-vault route are the two "escape hatches" an indoor oil-filled design normally lands on.

The cap is the design pivot: the same 5 MVA transformer at 34.5 kV can sit in a Type I/II room (with no storage, a confinement area, and the listing's restrictions), while the 38 kV version of the same unit must go into a 450.26 vault.
