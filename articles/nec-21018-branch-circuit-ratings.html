NEC 210.18 — Branch-Circuit Ratings

How a branch circuit gets its ampere rating — the OCPD (not the wire) sets the rating, the list of permitted ratings for multioutlet circuits (15/20/30/40/50 A; the 2023 NEC adds 10 A), and the >50 A multioutlet industrial exception. What 210.18 is (and is not)

210.18 answers one question: what is the ampere rating of this branch circuit? It sits between two other sections. 210.20 sizes the overcurrent device (the 125% continuous-load floor); 210.19(A) sizes the conductors (ampacity ≥ circuit rating). 210.18 does the bookkeeping in between: it takes the OCPD's rating and declares that to be the circuit's rating — and it polices which ratings may exist on a multioutlet circuit.

The section has two sentences and one exception, and they are not the same kind of rule:

  • Sentence 1 — the mechanism. A branch circuit is rated in accordance with the maximum permitted ampere rating or setting of the overcurrent device. The OCPD sets the rating. This is a definition, not a sizing rule — 210.18 never tells you what size breaker to use; it tells you what the circuit's rating is once the breaker exists.
  • Sentence 2 — the list. For other than individual branch circuits (i.e. multioutlet circuits), the rating shall be 15, 20, 30, 40, and 50 amperes (2017/2020) — 10, 15, 20, 30, 40, and 50 amperes in the 2023 NEC. This is a closed list. 25 A is a standard breaker rating (240.6) but not a permitted multioutlet circuit rating — a 25 A circuit must be an individual branch circuit (one utilization equipment).
  • Sentence 3 — the second half of the mechanism. Where conductors of higher ampacity are used for any reason, the ampere rating or setting of the specified overcurrent device shall determine the circuit rating. Bigger wire does not raise the rating — this sentence is 210.18's guard against "I ran 10 AWG on a 20 A circuit, so it must be a 35 A circuit." It is not.
  • Exception — the >50 A escape valve. Multioutlet branch circuits greater than 50 amperes shall be permitted to supply nonlighting outlet loads on industrial premises where (2017/2020) in locations where (2023) conditions of maintenance and supervision ensure that only qualified persons service the equipment. (2023 renumbers this as Exception No. 1 and adds a new Exception No. 2: a 10 A circuit shall not supply receptacle outlets.)

The practical rule: 210.18 is the source of truth for the number written next to a breaker on the panel schedule. When PanelWright writes "20 A" beside a breaker, that number is the 210.18 rating: it equals the OCPD rating (sentence 1), it must be one of the list values unless the circuit is individual (sentence 2), and it does not change if the feeder-grade wire in the bundle is bigger than required (sentence 3).

The mechanism at a glance

ExceptionEscape valveMultioutlet circuits > 50 A permitted to supply nonlighting outlet loads on industrial premises (2023: "in locations") where only qualified persons service the equipment.

Why the closed list exists: a branch-circuit rating is also the number that 210.21 uses to pick receptacle ratings, the number 210.23/210.24 use to cap permissible loads, and the number Table 310.16 sizing starts from. A closed list of ratings keeps the downstream tables finite. Individual circuits (one fixed load each) are exempt because their "load" is a single piece of equipment with its own article (424.3, 422.10, 424.23 …), so the list would just be in the way.

The key definitions (Article 100)

210.18's whole structure turns on two Article 100 definitions, quoted verbatim (NEC 2017 on disk):

Branch Circuit. The circuit conductors between the final overcurrent device protecting the circuit and the outlet(s). (CMP-2) Branch Circuit, Individual. A branch circuit that supplies only one utilization equipment. (CMP-2) Source: verbatim NEC 2017, official NFPA text on disk (nec2017_full.txt, Article 100 definitions, lines 5027–5035). The marginal "N " markers dropped; no wording changed.

Read those two together: a branch circuit is the run of conductors from the final OCPD (the breaker) to the outlets. If it feeds exactly one utilization equipment, it is individual — and 210.18's list stops applying. Everything else (two receptacles, a receptacle + a luminaire, the kitchen counter circuit) is "other than individual," and the list governs its rating.

How the rating flows (the mechanism, in sequence)

For a given circuit, the working sequence is:

  1. Identify the load type. One utilization equipment → individual (list does not apply). Two or more outlets/receptacles → other than individual (list applies).
  2. Size the OCPD for the load per 210.20 (the 125% continuous floor, the 240.4/240.3/210.21 ceilings).
  3. Apply 210.18. Circuit rating = OCPD rating. If the circuit is other-than-individual, the rating must be on the list (15/20/30/40/50; +10 in 2023, and 10 A with no receptacles). If the OCPD lands on a non-list size (e.g. 25 A) on a multioutlet circuit, that combination is not permitted — restructure as an individual circuit or change the OCPD.
  4. Size the conductors for the resulting circuit rating per 210.19(A) + Table 310.16, with the 240.4(D) small-conductor OCPD caps in play. If you use bigger wire than required, stop there — sentence 3 says the rating stays with the OCPD.

The trap in most real problems is step 3: people treat 25 A as "just a breaker size" and put receptacles on a 25 A circuit. 210.18 is the section that says no — a multioutlet circuit may not be rated 25 A, so that circuit must be individual (one fixed load) or re-rated to 20/30 A.

Where 210.18 fits

  • The OCPD twin — 210.20: 210.20 decides what the breaker size must be (the 125% floor, the ceilings). 210.18 then names the resulting number the circuit's rating. 210.20 is a sizing rule; 210.18 is the declaration.
  • The conductor twin — 210.19(A): 210.19(A) sizes the conductors for the rating that 210.18 declares. The 240.4(D) caps (14 AWG → 15 A, 12 AWG → 20 A, 10 AWG → 30 A) are where the wire size and the breaker size meet — see 240.4(D) (used in EX2, EX3, EX5).
  • The downstream consumers — 210.21 / 210.23 / 210.24: the 210.18 rating is the input to 210.21 (receptacle ratings, EX7), 210.23 (permissible loads on the circuit), and Table 210.24 (max loads per circuit rating). Change the rating and all three move.
  • The standard-size source — 240.6: the OCPD ratings 210.18 references come from Table 240.6(A) (verified in the core's STD_BREAKERS, 15…6000 A; 2017 on disk, lines 16129–16170: the inverse-time breaker list and "Additional standard ampere ratings for fuses shall be 1, 3, 6, 10, and 601").
  • The definitions — Article 100: "Branch Circuit" and "Branch Circuit, Individual" (quoted above) are what make the "other than individual" list work.
