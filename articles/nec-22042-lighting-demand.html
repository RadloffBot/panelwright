The rule in one sentence

For the general lighting load (general illumination) on a feeder or service under the standard method, you take the total connected lighting VA and apply Table 220.42’s demand factors tier by tier — each factor applies only to the slice of load that falls inside that tier — and the demand is the sum of those tier contributions. The factors are set by the occupancy type (dwelling, hospital, hotel/motel, warehouse, or all others). The demand factors do not apply when counting the number of lighting branch circuits, and they do not apply under the 220.82 optional method.

The code text (verbatim)

220.42 General Lighting

“The demand factors specified in Table 220.42 shall apply to that portion of the total branch-circuit load calculated for general illumination. They shall not be applied in determining the number of branch circuits for general illumination.”Source: NEC 2014 / 2020 full-code text (NFPA 70), verbatim — the section body is identical in both editions (see Sources & verification for the one 2020-scan OCR artifact noted and corrected).

The whole rule is short because the substance lives in the table. Two scope sentences matter in practice: the factors apply to the general illumination portion of the branch-circuit load (not receptacles, cooking, dryers, etc.), and they may not be used to reduce the number of branch circuits you must install.

How the tiers work (it’s not one flat factor)

Table 220.42 is a tiered table, not a single lookup. You split the connected lighting load into the tier bands and multiply each band by its own factor, then add the results. The most common error is applying one factor to the whole load. Example for a 150,000 VA dwelling lighting load: 3,000 @ 100% = 3,000; the 117,000 between 3,000 and 120,000 @ 35% = 40,950; the 30,000 over 120,000 @ 25% = 7,500; total demand = 51,450 VA — not 150,000 × 35%.

At each seam the demand is continuous (no jump) — crossing a boundary changes which factor applies to the next VA, not the VA at the boundary. PanelWright walks the tiers with cumulative upper bounds, so every seam is handled exactly, and these values are pinned by the public test suite.

Why this table (a source-quality note)

The verbatim NFPA 70 2014 Article 220 PDF (the table read directly from the print layout, clean text layer) is unambiguous, and it is what PanelWright implements. One 2020-edition OCR text source (the on-disk full-code scan) rendered the hotel/motel row as “60 / 50 / 35” instead of “50 / 40 / 30” — but that same OCR row also mangles the “100,000” tier boundary into “J00,000” / “l00,000,” the signature of the digit-garbling corruption class this project has hit before. Three independent live sources (VoltageLab, a 2026 code reference, and ecalpro) all state the hotel/motel factors as 20,000 @ 50%, 20,001–100,000 @ 40%, remainder @ 30% — matching the verbatim 2014 table and the shipped tool. If a free source prints a different hotel factor, the verbatim table wins.

Where 220.42 fits in a load calc

  • Standard method (Article 220, Part III): the 220.42 demand is the general illumination line of the feeder/service sum. It replaces the raw connected lighting total with the demanded value. The small-appliance (220.52(A)) and laundry (220.52(B)) loads may be added to the general lighting load and subjected to the same Table 220.42 factors.
  • Optional method (220.82): for a single dwelling unit under the optional method, general lighting enters at 3 VA per square foot at 100% (220.82(B)(1)) — the Table 220.42 demand factors do not apply on top of 220.82. The 220.82 card on the main page covers that case; do not double-count.
  • Not for circuit count: the factors reduce the load, not the number of general-illumination branch circuits. Size the circuit count from the full, undemanded lighting load.
  • Non-dwelling lighting: for non-dwellings the lighting load typically comes from the actual fixture schedule or a design VA/sq ft — enter the resulting total VA in the card. The card is a demand calculator on a total; it does not derive that total from floor area for you (the 3 VA/sq ft is a dwelling general-illumination unit value, not a 220.42 table value).

Editions: 2014 / 2017 / 2020 / 2023 / 2026

  • 2014: the section text and Table 220.42 in the form above, verified from the verbatim NFPA 70 2014 Article 220 text (read directly from the PDF, clean text layer).
  • 2017 / 2020: the section body is identical (the two verbatim editions were diffed this session). One 2020-scan OCR artifact in the hotel/motel table row (“60/50/35” + a garbled “100,000” boundary) was identified and corrected against the clean 2014 text and three live sources — disclosed in the source-quality note.
  • 2023: the published 2023 change analysis records no change to 220.42 (it appears only in index/overview references, not in change text).
  • 2026: the NEC renumbers load calculations out of Article 220 into the 120 area (220.42 moves toward 120.42) and a 2026 code reference indicates the dwelling general-illumination unit value (the 3 VA/sq ft) is revised in 2026. That unit-value change is not independently verified here and is outside the 220.42 demand table — PanelWright implements the 2014–2023 table it can cite, flags the renumber in the UI, and tells you to verify against the adopted edition.
