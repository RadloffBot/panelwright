NEC 230.90 — Where Required: Overload Protection for Service Conductors

Every ungrounded service conductor shall have overload protection. Where does it go — and can the service breaker be bigger than the wire?

The question this article answers

The load calc is done, the 230.79 service-disconnect article says what rating the disconnect must be, and the 230.42 article says how big the service-entrance conductors must be. But those are two different requirements — so who polices the match between the breaker and the wires? That is 230.90. It says: every ungrounded service conductor shall have overload protection, by an overcurrent device in series with it whose rating or setting is not higher than the conductor's ampacity — with five exceptions that loosen the rule where the code deliberately allows it (motor starts, the next-standard-size allowance, multi-breaker services, fire pumps, and the 83% dwelling-service rule). And 230.90(B) draws the one hard line nobody crosses: no overcurrent device in the service neutral.

230.90 — Where Required (2017)

230.90 Where Required. Each ungrounded service conductor shall have overload protection. (A) Ungrounded Conductor. Such protection shall be provided by an overcurrent device in series with each ungrounded service conductor that has a rating or setting not higher than the allowable ampacity of the conductor. A set of fuses shall be considered all the fuses required to protect all the ungrounded conductors of a circuit. Single-pole circuit breakers, grouped in accordance with 230.71(B), shall be considered as one protective device. Exception No. 1: For motor-starting currents, ratings that comply with 430.52, 430.62, and 430.63 shall be permitted. Exception No. 2: Fuses and circuit breakers with a rating or setting that complies with 240.4(B) or (C) and 240.6 shall be permitted. Exception No. 3: Two to six circuit breakers or sets of fuses shall be permitted as the overcurrent device to provide the overload protection. The sum of the ratings of the circuit breakers or fuses shall be permitted to exceed the ampacity of the service conductors, provided the calculated load does not exceed the ampacity of the service conductors. Exception No. 4: Overload protection for fire pump supply conductors shall comply with 695.4(B)(2)(a). Exception No. 5: Overload protection for 120/240-volt, 3-wire, single-phase dwelling services shall be permitted in accordance with the requirements of 310.15(B)(7). (B) Not in Grounded Conductor. No overcurrent device shall be inserted in a grounded service conductor except a circuit breaker that simultaneously opens all conductors of the circuit. Exception No. 4fire-pump supply conductors: 695.4(B)(2)(a)fire-pump services

The two most-missed pieces: Exception No. 3's load test — the combined rating may exceed the ampacity, but the calculated load may not, so the conductors still protect themselves against real demand — and (B), which is why a service disconnect is a 2-/3-/4-pole device and the neutral never rides on its own pole. Note what 230.90(A) does not do: it does not size the conductors (that is 230.42 →) and does not set the disconnect rating (that is 230.79 →). It polices the match between the two — and the 83% rule in Exception 5 is a conductor-sizing allowance (the conductors may be smaller than the breaker), not a permission to overload the conductors.

The exception the (A) sentence is built around: two-to-six breakers (or fuse sets) may carry the service, and their combined rating may exceed the conductor ampacity — provided the calculated load does not. The conductors remain the real limit on actual demand; the breakers only need to be able to open it. (The 2–6 count itself is the 230.71(B) territory the lead-in cross-references — see the 230.70–230.80 article →.)

The same rule at other system geometries: 3-phase 4-wire → 4-pole (all three ungrounded + the neutral share the simultaneous-open device — the neutral is still never on its own pole); 3-phase 3-wire (no neutral) → 3-pole. The neutral's size is governed by 220.61 (maximum unbalanced load) and the 310.12(D) smaller-neutral provision, not by any overcurrent device — see the 220.61 article →.

Where the service conductors feed a large motor (an equipment feed where the motor dominates), the starter's OCPD is set by the Article 430 tables, which routinely sit above the conductor ampacity for inrush — Exception No. 1 is what keeps that legal at the service level. Same architecture as 210.23(B)(1)/(D) and 430.52 on the branch/feeder side; the conductor-sizing article walks the feeder-side analog.

The non-standard-ampacity case: 2 AWG Cu at 75 °C carries 115 A, which no stock breaker matches. 240.4(B)/(C) (the next-standard-size rule, covered end-to-end in the conductor-sizing article →) lets you go up to the next 240.6(A) rating — 125 A — and 230.90(A) Exception No. 2 incorporates that same allowance at the service. Where 230.90 fits

  • The rating the OCPD must clear: the 230.70–230.80 service-disconnecting-means article → (230.79's rating floors — 15 / 30 / 100 one-family / 60 A — set the disconnect; 230.90(A) then ties that device to the conductor ampacity).
  • The conductor ampacity the OCPD must not exceed: the 230.42 service-conductor-sizing article → (230.42 sizes the wires; 230.90(A) is the OCPD-side check on those same wires).
  • The 83% rule's home: the 110.14(C) / 310.14 article → (the 2020 ampacity reorganization: 310.15 → 310.14, Table 310.15(B)(16) → Table 310.16, and 310.15(B)(7) → 310.12 — the renumber Exception 5 carries) and the 220.61 neutral-load article → (what the neutral actually carries; the 310.12(D) smaller-neutral provision).
  • The ampacity table the picks come from: the Table 310.16 ampacity article → — the 0.83 × rating math lands on its 75 °C column.
  • The load that sets everything upstream: the 220.82 optional dwelling service load article → and the standard-method cards; the calculated load is what Exception No. 3's test compares against the ampacity.
  • The next-standard-size allowance (Exception No. 2): the conductor-sizing article → (240.4 overcurrent end-to-end, including 240.4(B)/(C) and the 240.6(A) standard sizes).
  • 2026 NEC renumber flagged: the 2026 NEC reorganizes service and load-calculation material (Article 220 → the Article 120 area); verify 230.90's cross-references (310.12, 230.71(B), 240.4, 240.6, 430.52) against the adopted edition. This page cites 2017–2023 numbering.
