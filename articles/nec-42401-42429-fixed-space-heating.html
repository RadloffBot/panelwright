The 2017→2023 edition story — thirteen changed sections, machine-verified

The headline (Mike Holt-documented): 424.3 → 424.4 renumber + the continuous-load → 125% conductor reword. In 2017 the branch-circuit rules live in 424.3 Branch Circuits, and (B) is a flat declaration: "Fixed electric space-heating equipment and motors shall be considered continuous load." In 2023 the whole section is 424.4, and (B) becomes an explicit sizing rule: "The branch-circuit conductor(s) ampacity shall not be less than 125 percent of the load of the fixed electric space-heating equipment and any associated motor(s)." The Mike Holt 2023 change summary (on disk) documents exactly this: "424.4 Branch Circuits — The title and text of (B) were revised to clarify that this subdivision is about the sizing of the branch-circuit 'conductors.'" The practical effect is identical (a continuous load is sized at 125%), but 2023 states the 125% on the conductors directly instead of leaving it to the reader to apply the continuous-load definition. (Machine-verified: "shall be considered continuous load" is absent from every in-scope 2023 row; the 125% conductor sentence is present and absent from the 2017 scan.)
The Part I renumber cluster: 424.2 → 424.3 and 424.3 → 424.4. 2017's 424.2 "Other Articles" ("…shall also comply with Article 440") becomes 2023's 424.3, and the plain Article 440 sentence is replaced by a NEW Table 424.3 ("Air-conditioning and refrigerating equipment — 440 (Parts I, II, III, IV, V, VI)"), so a compressor-equipped heater now points at a table rather than a prose cross-reference. The branch-circuit section then shifts from 424.3 to 424.4. Two section numbers move in Part I; the content of the branch-circuit rules is otherwise carried forward (the (A) "for which they are rated" → "for which the branch circuit is rated" is the only (A) reword).
424.10 "Special Permission" DELETED. 2017 had a standalone 424.10: "Fixed electric space-heating equipment and systems installed by methods other than covered by this article shall be permitted only by special permission." 2023 has no such section — the 2023 424.10 is the baseboard-receptacle text (renumbered from 2017's 424.9). The special-permission catch-all is gone from the on-disk 2023 record (verified: "shall be permitted only by special permission" appears nowhere in the in-scope 2023 rows).
424.9 → 424.10 renumber + 210.50(B) → 210.52 cross-ref. The baseboard-heater receptacle rule (factory-installed receptacles in lieu of a required receptacle, not connected to the heater circuits) renumbers from 424.9 to 424.10, and the receptacle cross-reference moves from 210.50(B) to 210.52 (the 2023 dwelling-outlet rule number).
424.11 reword: "over 60°C insulation" → "insulation rating greater than 60°C." The supply-conductor marking rule is the same in substance (equipment needing >60 °C supply-conductor insulation must be clearly and permanently marked, visible after installation, permitted adjacent to the field connection box); only the phrasing tightens.
424.12(B) drops the water-accumulation sentence. 2017: damp/wet-location heaters "shall be listed for such locations and shall be constructed and installed so that water or other liquids cannot enter or accumulate in or on wired sections, electrical components, or ductwork." 2023 (B) ends after "shall be listed for such locations" — the listing requirement carries the protection, and the two Informational Notes (110.11 deteriorating agents; 680.27(C) pool decks) are retained. (A) is word-identical.
424.19 lead + 424.19(B)(1): the lockable clause is reworded. 2017 (both places): "…is lockable in accordance with 110.25." 2023 (both places): "…is capable of being locked in the open position in compliance with 110.25." Same 110.25 anchor, same two routes (in sight OR lockable-open) — the 2023 wording is the fuller phrase the rest of the Code uses for a lockable disconnect.
424.19(C)(1)/(C)(2): "control lamps and appliances" → "control general-purpose circuits and appliance circuits." The unit-switch-as-disconnect route's permission for the other disconnecting means to also control other loads is reworded from the 2017 "lamps and appliances" to the 2023 "general-purpose circuits and appliance circuits" — a tighter, more Code-precise description of what may be on the same switch. (C) lead, (C)(3) one-family, and (C)(4) other occupancies are word-identical.
424.20(A) gains a fifth condition: "Located in an accessible location." 2017's four conditions end at "(4) Located as specified in 424.19." 2023 adds "(5) Located in an accessible location" — a thermostat used as the disconnecting means must now sit in an accessible location, not merely be located per 424.19.
424.22(A) reword + 424.22(B) cross-ref renumber. 424.22(A): 2017 "other than such motor-operated equipment as required by Articles 430 and 440 … branch circuits in Article 210" → 2023 "other than motor-operated equipment required to have additional overcurrent protection by Parts III and IV of Article 430 or Parts III and VI of Article 440 … branch circuits in Part II of Article 210" (the cross-references get more specific). 424.22(B): the supplementary-device rating cross-reference renumbers 424.3(B) → 424.4(B) (following the Part I renumber). The 60 A / 48 A element-subdivision core, (D), and (E) are word-identical.
424.28(B): "visible or easily accessible" → "visible or accessible." The nameplate-location rule drops "easily" — the nameplate must be visible or accessible after installation. (A) is word-identical.

EX1 — the 424.4(B) 125% continuous-load conductor rule on a single large baseboard heater

A 12,000 W / 240 V baseboard heater on its own individual branch circuit. 424.4(A) permits an individual circuit to supply any rating for which it is rated; 424.4(B) (the 2023 reword) sizes the conductors at 125% of the load because it is a continuous load:

The 125% is the whole of 424.4(B): a 50 A nameplate load needs 62.5 A of conductor ampacity, which lands a 6 AWG Cu (65 A) with a 70 A OCPD. In 2017 the same number came from the "considered continuous load" declaration + 210.20(A) 125%; in 2023 the 125% is written directly onto the conductors.

Two 2,400 W / 240 V baseboard heaters (4,800 W total = 20 A continuous) share one branch circuit — two outlets for fixed electric space-heating. 424.4(A) caps such a circuit at 30 A:

The 30 A cap does not bind here (the 125% value is 25 A, already under 30 A) — it is a ceiling, not a floor. The real trap is 240.4(D): a 12 AWG Cu conductor is capped at a 20 A OCPD, so a 12 AWG cannot legally feed a 25 A OCPD. The core picks 12 AWG by ampacity (25 A ≥ 25 A required), but to pair with the 25 A OCPD this 20 A continuous load actually requires, step the conductor up to 10 AWG Cu (30 A @ 75 °C). The 240.4(D) 20 A cap is flagged by the shipped core itself.

Where 424.1–424.29 fit

  • The appliance-side neighbor: the NEC 422.10–422.13 appliance branch-circuit article (56) — 422.10/422.11 size the appliance circuit and OCPD and 422.13 makes the water heater a 125% continuous load; Article 424 is the fixed space-heating cousin (baseboard, duct, boiler, radiant) where the 125% continuous rule lives in 424.4(B) instead of 422.13.
  • The multi-motor hand-off: the NEC 430.24 + 430.53 several-motors article (57) — 430.24's conductor sum explicitly routes the space-heating load to 424.4(B) (the 125% continuous-load conductor rule this article documents); the 2017 424.3(B) cross-reference that 430.24 used is the one 2023 renumbers to 424.4(B).
  • The appliance disconnecting-means neighbor: the NEC 422.30–422.62 appliance disconnecting-means article (59) — 424.19(A)(2)(2) routes a motor-driven heater with a compliant unit switch to 422.34(A), (B), (C), or (D) (the out-of-sight allowance), the same unit-switch rules Article 424 leans on.
  • The air-conditioning / refrigerating cross-reference: the NEC 440.22 + 440.32 air-conditioning branch-circuit article (50) — 424.2 (2017) / 424.3 + Table 424.3 (2023) sends a compressor-equipped space heater to Article 440 (Parts I–VI), which is the source of the 175% / 125% / 72% rules that article works.
  • The OCPD catalog and the conductor table behind every rating above: the NEC 240.6 standard ampere ratings (the 70 A / 25 A / 50 A OCPD steps) and the Table 310.16 allowable ampacities (the 6 AWG / 12 AWG / 8 AWG picks), with the 240.4(D) small-conductor caps (the EX2 12 AWG → 20 A trap).
