NEC 422.16 + 422.18 + 422.22 — Appliance Flexible Cords & Ceiling-Fan Support

Article 422 Part I says how an appliance's branch circuit is sized (422.10–422.13, the previous article). This article is the Part II installation companion: 422.16 (which appliances may hang off a flexible cord, and the exact cord lengths / receptacle placement / plug type for each), 422.18 + 314.27(C)/(E) (how a ceiling-suspended paddle fan is supported — the section where 2023 renames the "locking support and mounting receptacle" to a weight-supporting ceiling receptacle (WSCR) + weight-supporting attachment fitting (WSAF) and adds a brand-new bathtub/shower zone), and 422.22 (the "other installation methods" catch-all that 2023 rewrites into a specific WSCR appliance-mounting path). Two sections — 422.15 (central vacuum) and 422.43 (flexible cords / heater cords) — were deleted outright in 2023. Thirteen verified deltas, all machine-checked, plus five worked examples from the shipped cores.

422.16 — which appliances hang off a cord, and how

422.16(A) — the general permission. Flexible cord is permitted for an appliance (1) to facilitate frequent interchange or to prevent noise/vibration, or (2) to allow ready removal of a fastened-in-place appliance whose fastening is specifically designed for it. 2023 adds a third bullet, (A)(3): the heater-cord rule. That requirement — cord-and-plug electrically-heated appliances that exceed 121 °C (250 °F) on the surface the cord touches must use a Table 400.4 heater cord — used to live in 422.43(A). 2023 deletes 422.43 and folds this sentence into 422.16(A)(3). (EX2's dishwasher and EX3's range hood are cord-and-plug; the heater-cord clause is what a space heater or iron would hit.)

422.16(B) — the specific appliances. Four named appliances, each with its own cord length, plug, and receptacle rules:

  • (B)(1) In-sink waste disposers — cord 450 mm (18 in.) to 900 mm (36 in.), grounding-type plug (double-insulation exception), receptacle protected from damage and accessible. (EX1.)
  • (B)(2) Built-in dishwashers + trash compactors — dishwasher cord 0.9 m (3 ft) to 2.0 m (6.5 ft), receptacle in the space adjacent to the dishwasher; compactor cord 0.9 m to 1.2 m (4 ft), receptacle in or adjacent to the occupied space. Both: grounding plug (double-insulation exception). 2023 adds cord-through-opening bushing protection. (EX2.)
  • (B)(3) Wall-mounted ovens + counter-mounted cooking units — may be permanently connected, or cord-and-plug "only for ease in servicing or for installation"; the separable connector/plug must be approved for the space temperature.
  • (B)(4) Range hoods — cord 450 mm (18 in.) to 1.2 m (4 ft), and the receptacle must be supplied by an individual branch circuit. 2023 expands the scope to include over-the-range microwave ovens with integral range hoods. (EX3.)
The 240.4(D) gate the cord rules trip. Every one of these is a small appliance on a 120 V or 240 V branch circuit, so the conductor is picked from Table 310.16 and then held to the 240.4(D) small-conductor OCPD caps (14 AWG Cu → 15 A, 12 AWG Cu → 20 A, 10 AWG Cu → 30 A). EX1 (5 A disposer) and EX2 (18.75 A dishwasher) show the gate both ways: EX1's 15 A OCPD lets 14 AWG survive (the 15 A cap equals the OCPD), while EX2's 20 A OCPD rejects 14 AWG even though it passes the 75 °C ampacity at exactly 20 A — because the 240.4(D) 15 A cap is below the 20 A device. The caps are worked in the 240.4(D) small-conductor article.

422.18 + 314.27 — how a paddle fan is supported (and the 2023 WSCR/WSAF rename)

422.18 is the support rule: the fan goes on an independent support, or on one of two listed hardware paths. The change in 2023 is a rename + a new zone:

  • 2017 (1) — a listed outlet box / box system "identified for the use" per 314.27(C).
  • 2017 (2) — a box system + a "listed locking support and mounting receptacle" + a compatible factory-installed attachment fitting, per 314.27(E).
  • 2023 (A)(1) — same box, now "identified for fan support".
  • 2023 (A)(2) — the "locking support and mounting receptacle" is renamed to a "listed weight-supporting ceiling receptacle (WSCR)" used with a "compatible weight-supporting attachment fitting (WSAF)", identified for the weight + mounting orientation of the listing, per 314.27(E).
  • 2023 (B) — NEW — the bathtub/shower zone (below).

314.27(C) — the box itself. Both editions: sole-support boxes are listed, marked by the manufacturer, and capped at 32 kg (70 lb); boxes over 16 kg (35 lb) must be marked with the max supported weight. The 2023 deltas: the marking is now "on the interior of the box" (2017 said "by their manufacturer"); a NEW habitable-room rule requires a dwelling fan-location box to be either listed for sole fan support or installed so you can reach structural framing through the box without removing it; and the 2017 spare-conductor sentence (spare separately-switched ungrounded conductors at a fan box in dwellings forced a sole-support listing) is gone — it is replaced by the two-option habitable-room rule. (EX4.)

314.27(E) — the separable fitting. 2017: "listed locking support and mounting receptacles used in combination with compatible attachment fittings." 2023: the same idea, now named WSCR + WSAF, with the box-fill hook (314.16(B)(4)) unchanged and a new Informational Note pointing to ANSI/NEMA WD-6 for standard WSCR/WSAF configurations. 2023 also lets a listed WSCR + WSAF serve as the sole support of a paddle fan per 314.27(C) — the bridge that makes 422.18(A)(2) work.

NEW in 2023 — 422.18(B) the bathtub/shower zone. "No metal parts of ceiling-suspended (paddle) fans in bathrooms and shower spaces shall be located within a zone measured 900 mm (3 ft) horizontally and 2.5 m (8 ft) vertically from the top of the bathtub rim or shower stall threshold. This zone is all-encompassing and shall include the space directly over the tub or shower stall." The 2017 edition has no (B) subdivision at all — 422.18 is the support rule only. The parallel air-conditioning zone is 440.8 (also new in 2023, worked in the air-cooling article). (EX5.)

422.22 — from "special permission" to a named WSCR path

2017 422.22 was the catch-all: "Appliances employing methods of installation other than covered by this article shall be permitted to be used only by special permission." 2023 422.22 is a specific permitted method: "Appliances shall be permitted to use listed weight-supporting ceiling receptacles in combination with compatible weight-supporting attachment fittings used within their ratings and used in accordance with 314.27(E)." The net effect: the WSCR/WSAF hardware that 2023 introduced for fans (422.18 / 314.27(E)) is now also an appliance mounting path — and the "special permission" framing is dropped. (This is the delta that pairs 422.22 with the 422.18/314.27(E) rename in one coherent 2023 rework.)

The teaching point is the 240.4(D) gate at the boundary. The ampacity pick is 14 AWG (15 A at 60 °C), and the 240.4(D) cap on 14 AWG Cu is exactly 15 A. Because the cap equals the 15 A OCPD, 14 AWG survives. Ampacity pick, 60 °C Cu column12 AWG Cu (20 A ≥ 18.75 A)
Answer12 AWG Cu (the 60 °C column gives 20 A; the 75 °C column gives 25 A — 12 AWG in both)

This is the gate EX1 only grazed. At 75 °C, 14 AWG Cu carries 20 A — enough for the 18.75 A load. But 240.4(D) caps 14 AWG Cu at 15 A of overcurrent protection, and the OCPD here is 20 A. A 15 A cap under a 20 A device is not allowed, so 14 AWG is rejected on the OCPD gate even though it passes ampacity. 12 AWG (20 A at 60 °C / 25 A at 75 °C) is the answer. The cord itself (0.9 m–2.0 m, receptacle in the adjacent space, plus the 2023 opening-bushing clause) is 422.16(B)(2) business — the circuit sizing is 210.23(A) / Table 310.16 / 240.4(D).

The range-hood rule's real constraint is 422.16(B)(4)(5): the receptacle must be supplied by an individual branch circuit — the hood can't share the countertop small-appliance circuit. The 2023 scope change: an over-the-range microwave with an integral range hood now qualifies for the same cord-and-plug treatment under these four conditions (2017 was "Range hoods" only). The cord length (450 mm–1.2 m) and the individual-circuit condition are unchanged across the two editions.

This rule exists only in 2023 — 2017 422.18 has no (B) subdivision. The zone is a box: 0.9 m (3 ft) out from the tub rim / stall threshold and 2.5 m (8 ft) up from the top of the rim, and it is all-encompassing (it includes the space directly over the tub). If a paddle fan's metal parts fall inside that box, they are not permitted. The first fan (2.6 m up) clears the 2.5 m vertical limit; the third (1.2 m out) clears the 0.9 m horizontal limit; the middle one is inside on both axes and fails. The parallel air-conditioning zone (440.8, also new in 2023) works the same way for AC equipment — see the air-cooling article. The bathroom receptacle / GFCI rules for the same space are in the 210.8 GFCI article.

Where 422.16 + 422.18 + 422.22 fit

  • The branch-circuit foundation this article extends: the NEC 422.10 + 422.11 + 422.12 + 422.13 appliance branch circuit — 422.10/422.11 size the circuit (125% continuous floor, 150% OCPD ceiling, the 20 A unmarked-appliance cap, the 48 A heater subdivision); 422.16/422.18/422.22 are the installation rules for the cord, the fan support, and the mounting that Part I leaves open.
  • The OCPD cap the cord examples trip: the NEC 240.4(D) small-conductor caps — EX1's 14 AWG / 15 A boundary and EX2's 14 AWG rejection (15 A cap under a 20 A OCPD) are both 240.4(D) in action.
  • The conductor table the picks come from: the Table 310.16 allowable ampacities (14 AWG = 15/20/25 A in the 60/75/90 °C columns; 12 AWG = 20/25/30 A) and the NEC 240.6 standard ampere ratings (the 18.75 A → 20 A step in EX2).
  • The parallel tub/shower zone for AC equipment: the NEC 440.22 + 440.32 air-cooling branch circuit — 440.8's new bathtub/shower zone (2023) is the air-conditioning twin of 422.18(B)'s paddle-fan zone.
  • The same space's receptacle protection: the NEC 210.8 GFCI-protection article — the 6 ft sink/tub distance tests and the dwelling bathroom-location list for the receptacles near the EX5 zone.
