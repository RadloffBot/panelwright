NEC 408 — Switchboards, Switchgear, and Panelboards

The article that answers "my feeder lands in a 200 A panelboard — how big does the panelboard have to be, how big is its overcurrent device, and what does the 2023 NEC now require me to mark about the fault current?" Article 408 is the equipment article behind every panel schedule: the 408.30 rule that the panelboard rating must not be less than the minimum feeder capacity (the exact number PanelWright's service rollup feeds), the 408.36 overcurrent-protection cap (OCPD rating not greater than the panelboard, the two-main-breaker 42-device rule, the 30 A snap-switch / 200 A rule), the 2023-new 408.6 short-circuit-current-rating rule (SCCR not less than the available fault current, field-marked with the calculation date), the 2023-new 408.9 replacement-panelboard rule (the 10,000 A field-label seam), the 2023-new 408.43 face-up/face-down prohibition, and the construction core (Tables 408.5 and 408.56). 408.36 Overcurrent Protection — the cap, the exceptions (restructured 2023), (A)–(D)

408.36 — 2017 (three exceptions, verbatim)
408.36 Overcurrent Protection. In addition to the requirement of 408.30, a panelboard shall be protected by an overcurrent protective device having a rating not greater than that of the panelboard. This overcurrent protective device shall be located within or at any point on the supply side of the panelboard. Exception No. 1: Individual protection shall not be required for a panelboard used as service equipment with multiple disconnecting means in accordance with 230.71. In panelboards protected by three or more main circuit breakers or sets of fuses, the circuit breakers or sets of fuses shall not supply a second bus structure within the same panelboard assembly. Exception No. 2: Individual protection shall not be required for a panelboard protected on its supply side by two main circuit breakers or two sets of fuses having a combined rating not greater than that of the panelboard. A panelboard constructed or wired under this exception shall not contain more than 42 overcurrent devices. For the purposes of determining the maximum of 42 overcurrent devices, a 2-pole or a 3-pole circuit breaker shall be considered as two or three overcurrent devices, respectively. Exception No. 3: For existing panelboards, individual protection shall not be required for a panelboard used as service equipment for an individual residential occupancy. (A) Snap Switches Rated at 30 Amperes or Less. Panelboards equipped with snap switches rated at 30 amperes or less shall have overcurrent protection of 200 amperes or less. (B) Supplied Through a Transformer. Where a panelboard is supplied through a transformer, the overcurrent protection required by 408.36 shall be located on the secondary side of the transformer. Exception: A panelboard supplied by the secondary side of a transformer shall be considered as protected by the overcurrent protection provided on the primary side of the transformer where that protection is in accordance with 240,21(C)(1). Frequently asked questions

Q: What is the difference between a switchboard, switchgear, and a panelboard?
Article 408 covers all three with shared rules (408.3 busbar support/arrangement, 408.16–408.22 switchboard/switchgear location and grounding) plus panelboard-specific Part III (408.30–408.43): the 408.30 rating floor (not less than the minimum feeder capacity per Article 220), the 408.36 OCPD cap, the dead-front 408.38 enclosure, the 408.40/408.41 grounding/termination rules, and the 2023-new 408.43 orientation prohibition. In practice: a panelboard is the load-side equipment full of branch/feeder OCPDs; switchboards and switchgear are the larger building services (Part II/IV rules, Table 408.56 spacings). The code text is quoted verbatim above — this page is a design aid, not a definition manual.
Q: What size overcurrent device do I use for a panelboard?
408.36: a panelboard "shall be protected by an overcurrent protective device having a rating not greater than that of the panelboard," located within or on the supply side. Two 2023 exceptions: (1) two main breakers/fuse sets in other than service equipment with a combined rating ≤ the panelboard — capped at 42 overcurrent devices (2-pole counts as two, 3-pole as three); (2) existing panelboards used as service equipment for an individual residential occupancy. Plus 408.36(A): snap switches ≤ 30 A → OCPD ≤ 200 A; (B): transformer-supplied → protection on the secondary; (C): no 3-phase OCPD on a less-than-3-phase bus, no delta breakers in panelboards; (D): backfed plug-in devices secured with an additional fastener.
Q: What is the new 408.6, and what does it make me mark?
New in 2023: switchboards, switchgear, and panelboards "shall have a short-circuit current rating not less than the available fault current." Other than in one- and two-family dwelling units, the available fault current and the date the calculation was performed "shall be field marked on the enclosure at the point of supply," per 110.21(B)(3). The companion 10,000 A rules (408.9(B) field-label, 408.38 evaluation) are the same number applied to replacements and enclosures.
Q: Can I put a new panelboard "guts" set into an old enclosure?
Yes — 408.9 (new in 2023) governs: if the replacement panelboard is listed for the specific enclosure (catalog number or dimensional information), it "shall be permitted to maintain its short-circuit current rating." If the available fault current is greater than 10,000 A, the completed work "shall be field labeled"; at 10,000 A or less, the replacement panelboard "shall be identified for the application." Either way, "any previously applied listing marks on the cabinet that pertain to the panelboard shall be removed."
Q: Can I install a panelboard face-up or face-down?
No. 408.43 (new in 2023): "Panelboards shall not be installed in the face-up or face-down position." The on-disk Mike Holt 2023 summary documents this as the panelboard-orientation rule.
Q: What changed in Article 408 from 2017 to 2023?
