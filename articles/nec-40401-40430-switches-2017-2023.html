EX1 - the 404.14(D) push-in-terminal corner (NEW in 2023) + the 20 A comparison

A 15 A branch circuit terminated in a screwless push-in snap switch. 404.14(D): push-in terminals "shall be installed on not greater than 15-ampere branch circuits and shall be connected with 14 AWG solid copper wire only" unless listed for other conductors. Machine check from the shipped core: a 15 A OCPD is exactly at the branch cap (15 ≤ 15, passes); the 75 °C ampacity pick for 15 A is 14 AWG Cu, and 240.4(D) caps 14 AWG Cu at 15 A = the OCPD - the 15 A / 14 AWG pair is the legal maximum for this termination type (the same corner 406.3(D) creates for receptacles). The 20 A comparison: a 20 A branch cannot use a push-in snap switch at all (20 > 15, over the branch cap); its 75 °C pick is 14 AWG Cu, and its 240.4(D)-compliant pick is 12 AWG Cu (cap 20 A = the 20 A OCPD).

core: nextStdBreaker(15) = 15 A; pickConductor31016(15, "cu", 75) = 14 AWG; smallConductorCap(14, "cu") = 15 A; pickConductor31016(20, "cu", 75) = 14 / pickConductor31016(20, "cu", 60) = 12 AWG; smallConductorCap(12, "cu") = 20 A

EX2 - the 404.14(A)/(B) load caps on a 20 A switch

A 20 A general-use snap switch. 404.14(A)(3): motor loads not exceeding 80 percent of the ampere rating = 16 A. 404.14(B)(2): inductive loads (AC/DC form) not exceeding 50 percent = 10 A. So a 16 A motor passes the (A) form; an 11 A inductive load fails the (B) form's 10 A cap. The 2017 scan and the 2023 row agree on both percentages (machine-verified).

80% x 20 A = 16 A; 50% x 20 A = 10 A (direct arithmetic on the on-disk percentages)

EX3 - the 404.14(G) OCPD floor for cord-and-plug-connected loads

A snap switch controlling receptacle outlets on a general-purpose branch circuit with a 20 A OCPD (nextStdBreaker(20)). 404.14(G): the switch "shall be rated at not less than the rating of the maximum permitted ampere rating or setting of the overcurrent device protecting the receptacles or cord connectors" - so the switch rating floor is 20 A (a 15 A snap switch on this circuit fails; the Exception for not-more-than-one-receptacle circuits allows rating at not less than the receptacle rating). The 2017 rule is substantively identical (disclosed artifacts only).

core: nextStdBreaker(20) = 20 A = switch rating floor (404.14(G))

Frequently asked questions

Q: What does Article 404 actually cover?
Switches and switching devices used as switches at 1000 volts and below: switch connections (404.2, incl. the 404.2(C) grounded-conductor rule for lighting switch boxes), enclosures (404.3 + the 404.28 Table 312.6(B)(2) wire-bending space), damp/wet locations (404.4), time switches/flashers (404.5), knife-switch position and de-energized-blade connection (404.6), indicating (404.7), accessibility and grouping (404.8, incl. the 300 V adjacent-device limit), general-use snap-switch provisions (404.9-404.10), circuit breakers as switches (404.11), grounding of enclosures (404.12), knife-switch ampere seams (404.13), ratings and use (404.14, incl. the 2023-new 404.14(D) terminations), reconditioned switches (404.16, new in 2023), marking (404.20), electronic control switches (404.22), auxiliary contacts (404.26), fused switches (404.27), and the 2023-new 404.30 closed-door interior access. Branch-circuit rules live in Article 210; this article is what makes the switch at the end of each circuit legal.
Q: What changed in Article 404 from 2017 to 2023?
29 machine-diffed deltas (table above). The substance: 404.1 now excludes wireless control equipment (Mike Holt-documented); 404.2(C) rewords the location list, deletes the January 1, 2020 effective-date sentence, and drops one no-grounded-conductor condition (the 5/25 electronic-switch counts stay); 404.7 rewords the indicating-location language; 404.8(A) converts three Exception No. items to an "except as follows" list; 404.9(B) reworks grounding (grounded → bonded, the two-method connection list); 404.10 extends the snap-switch rules to dimmers/control switches; 404.11's note expands; 404.12 re-cites 314.3; 404.13's notes reword; 404.14 is the big rework (LED/ballast loads added to (A)/(B)/(E), (C) simplified, NEW (D) terminations, (F) extended); NEW 404.16 reconditioned switches; 404.22 rewords to "electronic control switches"; 404.28 re-cites Table 312.6(B)(2); NEW 404.30 closed-door interior access. Several "deltas" are disclosed OCR misreads in the 2017 scan (four way, threecircuit, ground-faull, permitied, permatted, tap, ave, Motorcircuit, shail, trated) - they are flagged as such, not code changes.
Q: What is the new 404.14(D) termination rule?
Terminals of 15/20 A snap switches not marked CO/ALR take copper and copper-clad aluminum only; CO/ALR-marked terminals take copper, aluminum, and copper-clad aluminum; and screwless push-in terminals shall be installed on not greater than 15-ampere branch circuits and connected with 14 AWG solid copper wire only, unless listed for other conductors. The 15 A / 14 AWG pair is the legal maximum for a push-in snap switch - a 20 A branch cannot use a push-in snap switch at all. The companion 406.3(D) carries the same rule for receptacles. Mike Holt documented this as one of the two 2023 Article 404 changes.
Q: Which reconditioned switches are banned in 2023 (404.16)?
Brand-new section (zero 404.16 in the on-disk 2017 scan): reconditioned lighting/dimmer/electronic control switches (A) and reconditioned snap switches of any type (B) - not permitted. Reconditioned knife switches, switches with butt contacts, and bolted pressure contact switches (C) - permitted, but equipment damaged by fire, products of combustion, corrosive influences, or water must be specifically evaluated by its manufacturer or a qualified testing laboratory before returning to service. Reconditioned molded-case switches (D) - not permitted. Mike Holt documented neither 404.16 nor 404.30 in the on-disk 2023 change summary (machine-counted silence).
