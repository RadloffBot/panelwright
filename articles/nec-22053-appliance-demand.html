The rule in one sentence

For dwelling units (one-family, two-family, or multifamily), you may count the combined nameplate load of four or more fastened-in-place appliances at 75% — instead of 100% — when those appliances are served by the same feeder or service and each is rated ¼ hp or greater, or 500 watts or greater (2020+ wording). Fewer than four eligible appliances: no reduction — the full nameplate load counts.

The code text (verbatim)

2020 NEC — 220.53 Appliance Load — Dwelling Unit(s)

"It shall be permissible to apply a demand factor of 75 percent to the nameplate rating load of four or more appliances rated 1/4 hp or greater, or 500 watts or greater, that are fastened in place, and that are served by the same feeder or service in a one-family, two-family, or multifamily dwelling. This demand factor shall not apply to: (1) Household electric cooking equipment that is fastened in place (2) Clothes dryers (3) Space heating equipment (4) Air-conditioning equipment"Source: NEC 2020 full-code text (NFPA 70), as verified against the on-disk verbatim copy used to build the PanelWright 220.53 core function.

2017 NEC — same section, pre-2020 wording

"It shall be permissible to apply a demand factor of 75 percent to the nameplate rating load of four or more appliances fastened in place, other than electric ranges, clothes dryers, space-heating equipment, or air-conditioning equipment, that are served by the same feeder or service in a one-family, two-family, or multifamily dwelling."Source: 2017 code language as published by Electrical License Renewal (electricallicenserenewal.com), §220.53; identical pre-2020 wording appears in the NFPA 70 2014 Article 220 text.

What changed: the 75%-for-four-or-more core is identical in 2014, 2017, and 2020. The 2020 edition (a) added the rating qualifier — each appliance must be rated ¼ hp or greater, or 500 W or greater — and (b) restated the exclusions as a numbered list. The qualifier matters in practice: a cord-and-plug desk fan or a 250 W garbage disposal is not an eligible 220.53 appliance and belongs in the general lighting/general-use receptacle load instead. PanelWright implements the 2017–2023 wording as written in the 2020 text.

What is excluded from the 75% factor

Four categories never count toward the 220.53 demand — each has its own, more specific rule:

Excluded loadWhere it belongs instead
Household electric cooking equipment, fastened in place (ranges, wall ovens, counter units)220.55 + Table 220.55 (Column C + Notes 1/2/3) — PanelWright's cooking appliance card
Clothes dryers220.54 + Table 220.54 (multi-dwelling standard method) or the 220.82(B)(3) nameplate treatment under the optional method
Space-heating equipment220.51 (fixed electric space heating at 100%) or 220.82(C) selections under the optional method
Air-conditioning equipment220.82(C)(1) (100% of nameplate) under the optional method

Typical eligible appliances are the permanently connected, fastened-in-place loads people forget to itemize: water heaters, dishwashers, washers (when on a dedicated circuit), garbage disposals above the rating threshold, bathroom fans, attic fans, and similar fixed loads — each rated ¼ hp or 500 W or greater (2020+ wording).

The math — three worked examples

There is no table to look up: 220.53 is one clean rule. Add the nameplate VA of every eligible appliance on the feeder/service, then apply the factor by count.

Notes: (1) exactly 4 appliances is eligible — the rule says "four or more." (2) 1,010 VA × 75% = 757.5 VA; the tool keeps two decimals and rounds nothing else. (3) 0 eligible appliances → 0 VA demand, no matter what total you might have mistyped. Where 220.53 fits in a dwelling load calc

  • Standard method (Article 220 Part III): general lighting (220.12/220.42) + small-appliance & laundry loads (220.52) + the 220.53 fixed-appliance demand here + cooking (220.55) + dryers (220.54) + space heat (220.51), then Table 220.42 demand on the lighting portion. The 220.53 line is the "misc fixed appliances" line of that sum.
  • Optional method (220.82): fixed appliances enter at 100% nameplate under 220.82(B)(3)(a) — "all appliances that are fastened in place, permanently connected, or located to be on a specific circuit" — and the 220.53 75% factor does not apply on top of 220.82's own 100%-first-10-kVA / 40%-of-remainder demand. Don't double-count: if you're using 220.82, the fixed appliances ride inside its (B)(3) nameplate total.
  • Same feeder or service: the factor applies to appliances served by the same feeder or service. Split loads across separate feeders, and each feeder gets its own 220.53 evaluation.

Editions: 2014 / 2017 / 2020 / 2023 / 2026

  • 2014: 75% for "four or more appliances fastened in place," exclusions written inline ("other than electric ranges, clothes dryers, space-heating equipment, or air-conditioning equipment"). No rating qualifier.
  • 2017: same rule, same pre-2020 wording.
  • 2020: adds the rating qualifier (¼ hp or 500 W or greater) and the numbered four-item exclusion list. This is the wording PanelWright implements.
  • 2023: the published change analysis records no change to 220.53.
  • 2026: the NEC renumbers load calculations out of Article 220 into the 120 area (220.53 moves toward the 120.53 region). Verify section numbers against the edition adopted where your work is inspected.
