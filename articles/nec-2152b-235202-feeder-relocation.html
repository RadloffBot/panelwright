The one thing that will confuse you if you're holding a 2017 code and a 2023 code

The over-600-volt feeder rules do not live in Article 215 in the 2023 NEC. In 2017, "Feeders over 600 Volts" was 215.2(B) — a subsection inside Article 215, right next to the ≤600 V rules. By 2023, that entire over-1000-V feeder set has been moved into the new Article 235, Part III, and the headline section is now 235.202. If a 2017 drawing or spec cites "215.2(B)" for a medium-voltage feeder, the correct 2023 cross-reference is "235.202".

That move did not happen all at once. It took two editions. In 2020 the section was still 215.2(B), but its heading changed from "Feeders over 600 Volts" to "Feeders over 1000 Volts" (the 600→1000 V migration) and its ampacity citations changed from 310.15/310.60 to 310.14/315.60. In 2023, the already-1000-V section was relocated out of Article 215 into Article 235 Part III and renumbered to 235.202, with its three subsections becoming 235.202(A)/(B)/(C). This article documents all three states, verbatim, and pins every change to the edition it landed in.

The centerpiece: 215.2(B) in 2017 — "Feeders over 600 Volts"

(B) Feeders over 600 Volts. The ampacity of conductors shall be in accordance with 310.15 and 310.60 as applicable. Where installed, the size of the feeder-circuit grounded conductor shall not be smaller than that required by 250.122, except that 250.122(F) shall not apply where grounded conductors are run in parallel. Feeder conductors over 600 volts shall be sized in accordance with 215.2(B)(1), (B)(2), or (B)(3).

(1) Feeders Supplying Transformers. The ampacity of feeder conductors shall not be less than the sum of the nameplate ratings of the transformers supplied when only transformers are supplied.

(2) Feeders Supplying Transformers and Utilization Equipment. The ampacity of feeders supplying a combination of transformers and utilization equipment shall not be less than the sum of the nameplate ratings of the transformers and 125 percent of the designed potential load of the utilization equipment that will be operated simultaneously.

(3) Supervised Installations. For supervised installations, feeder conductor sizing shall be permitted to be determined by qualified persons under engineering supervision. Supervised installations are defined as those portions of a facility where all of the following conditions are met: (1) Conditions of design and installation are provided under engineering supervision. (2) Qualified persons with documented training and experience in over 600-volt systems provide maintenance, monitoring, and servicing of the system.

Source: NEC 2017 (215.2(B)), verbatim from the on-disk 2017 scan (lines ~11175–11245). The scan prints the routing as "215.2(B) (1), (B) (2), or (B) (3)" with extra spaces — a layout artifact, not a real difference. Three things to notice: the ampacity tables cited are 310.15 and 310.60; the supervised route (B)(3) has no ampacity-table reference (it is engineering-supervision only); and the supervised definition keys to "over 600-volt systems."

The 2020 transition: same section, 1000 V, new tables

In the 2020 edition the section was still 215.2(B) — but it had already absorbed the 600→1000 V migration and the ampacity-table renumber. Two changes, both visible verbatim:

(B) Feeders over 1000 Volts. The ampacity of conductors shall be in accordance with 310.14 and 315.60 as applicable. Where installed, the size of the feeder-circuit grounded conductor shall not be smaller than that required by 250.122, except that 250.122(F) shall not apply where grounded conductors are run in parallel. Feeder conductors over 1000 volts shall be sized in accordance with 215.2(B)(1), (B)(2), or (B)(3).

Source: NEC 2020 (215.2(B)), verbatim from the on-disk 2020 scan. OCR disclosure: the scan prints the ampacity citation as "310.14 and 311.60" — the "311.60" is a scan artifact for the real citation 315.60 (the over-1000-V ampacity table), which the clean 2023 CSV confirms. So the 2020 deltas vs 2017 are: heading 600→1000 V, ampacity tables 310.15→310.14 and 310.60→315.60, and "over 600 volts" → "over 1000 volts" throughout. The three subsections (1)/(2)/(3) are unchanged except (3) gained its ampacity reference (below).

(3) Supervised Installations. For supervised installations, feeder conductor sizing shall be permitted to be determined by qualified persons under engineering supervision in accordance with 310.14(B) or 315.60(B). Supervised installations are defined as those portions of a facility where all of the following conditions are met: (1) Conditions of design and installation are provided under engineering supervision. (2) Qualified persons with documented training and experience in over 1000-volt systems provide maintenance, monitoring, and servicing of the system.

Source: NEC 2020 (215.2(B)(3)), verbatim from the on-disk 2020 scan (printed "310.14(B) or 311.60(B)" — the "311.60(B)" is the same OCR artifact for 315.60(B)). This is the edition that added the ampacity-table reference to the supervised route — 2017's (B)(3) had none. And "over 600-volt systems" became "over 1000-volt systems."

The 2023 destination: 235.202 — "Feeder conductors over 1000 volts"

In 2023 the section is no longer in Article 215. It is 235.202 in the new Article 235 Part III ("Feeders over 1000 Volts, Nominal"). Part III's scope statement, verbatim from the 2023 CSV:

235.201 Scope. Part III covers the installation requirements, overcurrent protection requirements, minimum size, and ampacity of conductors for feeders over 1000 volts ac or 1500 volts dc, nominal.

And the relocated sizing section itself. The lead sentence is word-identical to the 2020 215.2(B) lead (modulo the cleaned 315.60 citation):

The ampacity of conductors shall be in accordance with 310.14 and 315.60 as applicable. Where installed, the size of the feeder-circuit grounded conductor shall not be smaller than that required by 250.122, except that 250.122(F) shall not apply where grounded conductors are run in parallel. Feeder conductors over 1000 volts shall be sized in accordance with 235.202(A), (B), or (C).

(A) The ampacity of feeder conductors shall not be less than the sum of the nameplate ratings of the transformers supplied when only transformers are supplied.

(B) The ampacity of feeders supplying a combination of transformers and utilization equipment shall not be less than the sum of the nameplate ratings of the transformers and 125 percent of the designed potential load of the utilization equipment that will be operated simultaneously.

(C) For supervised installations, feeder conductor sizing shall be permitted to be determined by qualified persons under engineering supervision in accordance with 310.14(B) or 315.60(B). Supervised installations are defined as those portions of a facility where all of the following conditions are met: Conditions of design and installation are provided under engineering supervision. Qualified persons with documented training and experience in over 1000-volt systems provide maintenance, monitoring, and servicing of the system.

Source: NEC 2023 (235.202, (A), (B), (C)), verbatim from the on-disk 2023 NEC CSV. Note (A) and (B) are word-identical to the 2017 215.2(B)(1) and (B)(2) — the rule content never changed, only the section number and the surrounding edition. (C) carries the 2020-added ampacity reference, now cleanly printed as 315.60(B).

Other Article 215 deltas, 2017 → 2023, edition-pinned

Beyond the relocation and the 215.2 relettering, these are the other changes I verified against the on-disk text, each pinned to the edition it landed in:

  • 215.1 (Scope) — 2023: gained the voltage cap. 2017/2020: "…ampacity of conductors for feeders." (no voltage limit; 2017 has an electrolytic-cell Exception). 2023: "…for feeders not over 1000 volts ac or 1500 volts dc, nominal. Informational Note: See Part III of Article 235 for feeders over 1000 volts ac or 1500 volts dc." This is the scope statement that formalizes the relocation.
  • 215.3 (Overcurrent) — 2020: Exception No. 2 (the 600–1000 V / Part IX routing exception) was deleted in 2020, leaving a single unnumbered Exception. 2023 keeps the single Exception and drops the words "the provisions of." The 2023 text, verbatim: "Feeders shall be protected against overcurrent in accordance with Part I of Article 240. Where a feeder supplies continuous loads or any combination of continuous and noncontinuous loads, the rating of the overcurrent device shall not be less than the noncontinuous load plus 125 percent of the continuous load." (The deleted 2017 text routed 600–1000 V feeders to "Parts I through VII of Article 240" and over-1000 V feeders to "Part IX" — verified against the 2017 scan and the 2017 Article 240 part headings on disk. One source-quality note: the AJB 2020 change newsletter mis-quotes the deleted text as "Parts I through VIII … Part X"; the on-disk 2017 print text is "Parts I through VII … Part IX.")
  • 215.9 (GFCI for personnel) — 2020 & 2023: in 2017 limited to "15- and 20-ampere receptacle branch circuits"; in 2020 that limitation was dropped (applies to feeders generally); in 2023 the word "listed" was added. The 2023 text, verbatim: "Feeders shall be permitted to be protected by a listed ground-fault circuit interrupter installed in a readily accessible location in lieu of the provisions for such interrupters as specified in 210.8 and 590.6(A)."
  • 215.10 (Ground-Fault Protection of Equipment) — 2020 & 2023: (a) a new Exception No. 3 added in 2020 (temporary feeders without GFPE permitted for repair/maintenance/emergencies, not to exceed 90 days); (b) the phase-to-phase ceiling raised from "not exceeding 600 volts" (2017 + 2020) to "not exceeding 1000 volts" (2023) — this is the delta that makes 1000/577 V wye feeder disconnects require GFPE in 2023; (c) 2023 shortened the "The provisions of this section" lead to "This section" and the Info Note to "See 517.17." The 2023 text, verbatim: "Each feeder disconnect rated 1000 amperes or more and installed on solidly grounded wye electrical systems of more than 150 volts to ground, but not exceeding 1000 volts phase-to-phase, shall be provided with ground-fault protection of equipment in accordance with 230.95." — and the 2020-added Exception No. 3: "If temporary feeder conductors are used to connect a generator to a facility for repair, maintenance, or emergencies, ground-fault protection of equipment shall not be required. Temporary feeders without ground-fault protection shall be permitted for the time period necessary but shall not exceed 90 days."
  • 215.6 (Feeder EGC) — 2020: dropped "in accordance with the provisions of 250.134" and changed "the requirements of 250.32(B)" to "the requirements of 250.32."
  • 215.15 (Feeders) — NEW in 2023: barrier rule — "Barriers shall be placed such that no energized, uninsulated, ungrounded busbar or terminal is exposed to inadvertent contact … while servicing load terminations in panelboards, switchboards, switchgear, or motor control centers supplied by feeder taps in 240.21(B) or transformer secondary conductors in 240.21(C) when the disconnecting device … is in the open position." (Not in the 2017 or 2020 on-disk text.)
  • 215.18 (SPD for feeders) — NEW in 2023: (A)–(E) surge-protective-device block, including (E) "SPDs shall have a nominal discharge current rating (In) of not less than 10kA." (Not in the 2017 or 2020 on-disk text.)
  • 215.12 (Identification) — unchanged in substance: the (A)/(B)/(C) structure and the dc "more than 60 volts" polarity-identification rules are present in all three editions (2023 keeps 60 V for ≤1000 V feeders; the over-1000-V mirror is 235.212, which uses 1500 V dc).
Edition-of-origin for 215.15 and 215.18: both are absent from the on-disk 2017 scan and the on-disk 2020 scan (which cuts off at 215.12), so I can confirm they are new by 2023 but cannot pin whether they first appeared in the 2020 or 2023 cycle from the sources on disk. They are labeled "NEW in 2023" (i.e., present in 2023, absent from the earlier on-disk editions) — not "added in the 2023 cycle." This is a disclosed source boundary, not an assertion.

Worked examples (all computed by the shipped core under node)

Conductor picks from pickConductor31016 and breaker steps from nextStdBreaker in app.js (75 °C column). Transformer full-load current uses the standard nameplate formula I = kVA·1000 / (V·1.732). Every number below was produced by compute_art46.js under node — no hand math.

EX1 — 235.202(A): size from the sum of transformer nameplate ratings

A 4160 V feeder (over 1000 V, so 235.202 applies) supplying two 500 kVA transformers, transformers only.
Nameplate current at 4160 V = 500·1000 / (4160·1.732) = 69.40 A each.
235.202(A): ampacity ≥ sum = 2 × 69.40 = 138.80 A.
Core pick(138.80 A, Cu, 75 °C) = 1/0 AWG Cu (150 A).
[2017/2020: identical math under 215.2(B)(1); only the section number and table differ.]
The nameplate current is taken at the 4160 V feeder voltage (primary side). This is the headline (A) rule — sum the nameplate ratings, transformers only.

EX2 — 235.202(B): the relocated 125% rule

A 4160 V feeder supplying one 500 kVA transformer (69.40 A) plus 20 A of utilization equipment operated simultaneously.
235.202(B): ampacity ≥ 69.40 + 125% × 20 = 69.40 + 25 = 94.40 A.
Core pick(94.40 A, Cu, 75 °C) = 3 AWG Cu (100 A).
The 125% is applied to the simultaneously-operated utilization load, not the transformer (the transformer is taken at 100% nameplate).

EX3 — 235.202(C): the supervised route and its edition-pinned ampacity reference

A supervised over-1000-V feeder (all 235.202(C) conditions met).
2017 (215.2(B)(3)): sizing "determined by qualified persons under engineering supervision" — no ampacity-table reference.
2020 (215.2(B)(3)): adds "in accordance with 310.14(B) or 315.60(B)" (scan OCR "311.60(B)").
2023 (235.202(C)): "in accordance with 310.14(B) or 315.60(B)" (cleaned).
No conductor pick here — the whole point is that a qualified person under engineering supervision determines the size, anchored to the ampacity tables from 2020 onward.

EX4 — 215.2(C): the 55-A service-relative rule (renumbered from 215.2(A)(3))

A 50-A service (8 AWG Cu service conductors, 50 A) feeding a feeder that carries the total 35-A load.
215.2(C) [2017/2020: 215.2(A)(3)]: where the service conductors are ≤ 55 A and carry the total load, feeder ampacity ≥ the service conductor ampacity = 50 A (not the 35-A load).
Core pick(50 A, Cu, 75 °C) = 8 AWG Cu (50 A).
The rule text is word-identical across editions; only the subsection letter moved (A)(3)→(C).

EX5 — 215.2(B): the grounded-conductor floor per 250.122 (renumbered from 215.2(A)(2))

An 800 A feeder OCPD.
215.2(B) [2017/2020: 215.2(A)(2)]: the feeder-circuit grounded conductor shall not be smaller than the 250.122 size for the OCPD.
Table 250.122 row "800" = 1/0 Cu (verified against the on-disk 2023 CSV 250.122 row "800 1/0 3/0").
2023 reworded this subsection to "the equipment grounding conductor size required by 250.122" — same table, same floor, tighter wording.

EX6 — 215.10: the 2023 600→1000 V delta in action

A 1200 A feeder disconnect on a 1000/577 V solidly grounded wye (577 V to ground).
215.10 applies to disconnects ≥ 1000 A, >150 V to ground, "not exceeding [600 / 1000] volts phase-to-phase":
2017 + 2020 (ceiling "600 volts phase-to-phase"): 1000 V > 600 V → NO ground-fault protection required.
2023 (ceiling "1000 volts phase-to-phase"): 1000 V ≤ 1000 V → ground-fault protection of equipment REQUIRED per 230.95.
Core nextStdBreaker(1200 A) = 1200 A standard disconnect.
This is the most consequential numeric delta in the article: the same 1000/577 V feeder disconnect is legal without GFPE under 2017/2020 and non-compliant without it under 2023.

Gotchas

  • Don't cross-cite across editions. "215.2(B)" (2017/2020) is "235.202" (2023). A 2017/2020 spec citing 215.2(B)(1)/(2)/(3) for an MV feeder translates to 235.202(A)/(B)/(C) in 2023. The reverse also holds: 235.202 has no 2017 section number — its substance is the 2017 215.2(B) block.
  • The two 600→1000 V moves landed in different editions. The sizing-heading voltage (215.2(B) "over 600" → "over 1000 Volts") moved in 2020. The GFPE voltage (215.10 "not exceeding 600 V phase-to-phase" → "1000 V") moved in 2023. If you're updating a 1000/577 V design, check both: the conductors have been sized to the 1000 V rule since 2020, but the ground-fault-protection obligation only appeared in 2023.
  • Ampacity tables changed in 2020, not 2023. 310.15/310.60 (2017) → 310.14/315.60 (2020 and 2023). If a 2017 calc references 310.15 or 310.60 for an over-1000-V feeder, re-run it against 310.14/315.60 — the table numbers for large conductors differ between the two table sets.
  • 235.202(A) and (B) are word-identical to 2017 — but 235.202(C) is not. The transformer (A) and 125% (B) rules never changed. The supervised (C) route gained its "in accordance with 310.14(B) or 315.60(B)" ampacity anchor in 2020. Don't assume all three subsections are frozen.
  • The 215.2 subsection letters moved in 2023. 2017/2020 215.2(A)(2) (grounded conductor) is 2023 215.2(B); 2017/2020 215.2(A)(3) (55-A rule) is 2023 215.2(C). A 2017 drawing citing "215.2(A)(3)" for the 55-A rule is now "215.2(C)".
  • 215.15 and 215.18 are new by 2023, but the exact cycle isn't provable from the sources on disk. Both are absent from the 2017 and 2020 on-disk text, so I confirm they exist in 2023 and not earlier on disk — I do not claim which cycle (2020 or 2023) introduced them. The 2020 scan cuts off at 215.12.
  • The AJB 2020 newsletter mis-quotes the deleted 215.3 Exception 2. It prints "Parts I through VIII … Part X" of Article 240; the actual 2017 print text (verified against the 2017 scan and the 2017 Article 240 part headings on disk) is "Parts I through VII … Part IX." Trust the on-disk print, not the secondary newsletter, for that citation.

The method, step by step

  1. Determine whether the feeder is over 1000 V ac / 1500 V dc, nominal. If so, in the 2023 edition apply 235.202 (Article 235 Part III), not Article 215. If ≤1000 V, apply 215.2(A) (the math in the 215.2 ampacity article).
  2. Translate any legacy 2017/2020 "215.2(B)" citations to 2023 "235.202" using the relocation map above.
  3. Size the conductors per 235.202: (A) sum of transformer nameplate ratings (transformers only); (B) nameplate ratings + 125% of simultaneously-operated utilization; or (C) qualified persons under engineering supervision (anchored to 310.14(B)/315.60(B) since 2020). Use the 315.60 ampacity table (310.14 where applicable).
  4. Size the grounded conductor ≥ 250.122 (250.122(F) parallel exception applies).
  5. Provide overcurrent protection per 235.203 (over-1000-V) / 215.3 (≤1000 V) — 125% of continuous load.
  6. Apply 215.10 ground-fault protection of equipment where the disconnect is ≥1000 A on a >150 V-to-ground wye ≤1000 V phase-to-phase (2023 ceiling; 600 V in 2017/2020) per 230.95.
  7. Apply the 55-A rule (215.2(C)) where a ≤55 A service carries the total feeder load.
  8. Verify against the adopted edition in your jurisdiction (2017/2020/2023 documented here; 2026 changes out of scope).

FAQ

Where did the over-600-V feeder rules go in the 2023 NEC?

In 2017 they were 215.2(B) "Feeders over 600 Volts", inside Article 215. In 2020 the heading became "Feeders over 1000 Volts" but it stayed at 215.2(B). In 2023 the whole over-1000-V feeder set moved to the new Article 235 Part III: 215.2(B)(1)→235.202(A), (B)(2)→235.202(B), (B)(3)→235.202(C). Article 215 in 2023 covers feeders "not over 1000 volts ac or 1500 volts dc, nominal" only.

How do you size a feeder that supplies only transformers?

235.202(A) (2017: 215.2(B)(1)): ampacity not less than the sum of the nameplate ratings of the transformers supplied when only transformers are supplied. Word-identical in all three editions. Sum the nameplate full-load currents and pick conductors from the ampacity table to meet that total.

What is the 125 percent rule in 235.202(B)?

For a feeder supplying transformers AND utilization equipment: ampacity ≥ sum of transformer nameplate ratings + 125% of the designed potential load of the utilization equipment operated simultaneously. The 125% mirrors continuous-load logic (simultaneous load treated as continuous). Word-identical across editions (2017 215.2(B)(2)).

What changed in the supervised-installation rule (235.202(C))?

2017 (215.2(B)(3)) had no ampacity-table reference. 2020 added "in accordance with 310.14(B) or 315.60(B)" (scan prints 311.60(B) OCR). 2023 cleaned it to 315.60(B). And "over 600-volt systems" became "over 1000-volt systems."

Why does a 1000-V wye feeder need GFPE in 2023 but not 2017?

215.10's phase-to-phase ceiling was "not exceeding 600 volts" in 2017 and 2020, and "not exceeding 1000 volts" in 2023. A 1200 A disconnect on a 1000/577 V wye is above the 600 V ceiling (no GFPE) under 2017/2020 but at/under the 1000 V ceiling (GFPE required, per 230.95) under 2023.

What is the 55-ampere rule, and why did its number change?

215.2(C) (2023): feeder ampacity not less than the service conductor ampacity where the service conductors (≤55 A) carry the total feeder load. In 2017/2020 it was 215.2(A)(3). The 2023 restructuring relettered it (A)(3)→(C); the rule text is word-identical.
