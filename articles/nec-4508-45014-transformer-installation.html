NEC 450.8–450.14 — Transformer Installation

Article 51 covered the overcurrent-protection core of Article 450 (450.3 / 450.4 / 450.5 / 450.6 / 450.7). This article is the rest of Part I General Provisions — the installation rules the OCPD core assumes you already satisfy: 450.8 guarding (mechanical protection, the noncombustible case, 110.27/110.34 guarding, voltage warning), 450.9 ventilation (full-load heat, unblocked openings, marked clearances), 450.10 grounding (the terminal bar inside the enclosure, 250.12 bonding, the 250.8 wire-lead Exception, other metal parts per 250 Parts V–VII), 450.11 marking (the eight required nameplate items), 450.12 terminal wiring space (312.6 bending space + the Table 314.16(B)(1) pigtail volume), 450.13 accessibility (the 50 kVA hollow-space boundary), and 450.14 disconnecting means (in sight or remote-lockable-open; Class 2 / Class 3 exempt). Together with 450.21–450.28 (dry-type / liquid-filled specifics, a future article) and Part III (transformer vaults), these rules decide where a transformer may physically live and how you reach it.

450.8 — Guarding

Four sub-rules, each doing one job:

  • (A) Mechanical Protection. Where a transformer is exposed to physical damage (a loading dock, a warehouse aisle, a parking structure), make provisions to minimize it. This is the catch-all the AHJ invokes when the transformer is in a high-traffic spot.
  • (B) Case or Enclosure. Dry-type transformers must be in a noncombustible, moisture-resistant case or enclosure that keeps fingers and tools out — “protection against the accidental insertion of foreign objects.” This is why a bare dry-type winding sitting in an open wall chase fails: the enclosure is part of the requirement, not an option.
  • (C) Exposed Energized Parts. Switches or other equipment at 1000 V nominal or less that serves only equipment inside the transformer enclosure may live inside it — but only if accessible to qualified persons only. Everything energized is still guarded per 110.27 (guards for 50 V–1000 V) and 110.34 (guards for 1000 V+).
  • (D) Voltage Warning. The operating voltage of exposed live parts must be indicated by signs or visible markings on the equipment or structures. For a 480/208 pad-mount with an open secondary disconnect, that means a “480 V” marking where the parts are exposed.

450.9 — Ventilation

One sentence does the work: the ventilation must dispose of the full-load heat losses without a temperature rise above the transformer rating. Informational Note No. 2 is the nonlinear-load warning — nonsinusoidal currents (VFDs, rectifiers, electronics) produce additional losses that heat the transformer above its rating without the OCPD ever seeing the load (see the Article 430 adjustable-speed-drive article → for the load side). The two installation rules: ventilating openings must not be blocked by walls or obstructions, and the required clearances must be clearly marked on the transformer (which is also why 450.11(A)(6) requires the clearances on the nameplate — the marking requirement is cross-referenced twice).

450.10 — Grounding (2023: “Grounding and Bonding”)

This is the section that ties the transformer into the separately-derived-system grounding story (see the 250.26–250.30 article → for 250.30, which 450.6(C) and the secondary grounding rules invoke):

  • (A) Dry-Type Transformer Enclosures. Where separate equipment grounding conductors and supply-side bonding jumpers are installed, a terminal bar for all grounding and bonding conductor connections is secured inside the enclosure, bonded to the enclosure per 250.12, and kept off any vented portion. The Exception is the practical one: where the transformer is equipped with wire-type connections (leads) — i.e. it has no terminal bar to begin with — the grounding and bonding connections may be connected together using any of the methods in 250.8 and bonded to the enclosure if of metal.
  • (B) Other Metal Parts. Exposed non-current-carrying metal parts — fences, guards, and so forth — are grounded and bonded per Parts V, VI, and VII of Article 250 (EGC sizing, equipment bonding, the bonding rules for structural metal). The 2017 text leads with the conditional “Where grounded, exposed …”; 2023 drops the conditional — the rule is unconditional.

450.12 — Terminal Wiring Space

Two different “space” rules, and they are not the same thing:

  • Bending space at the terminals — 312.6. The wire has to be able to bend from the raceway into the terminal without kinking or being pinched. For fixed, 1000-V-and-below transformer line and load connections, the minimum is the Table 312.6(A) gutter/bending space (one wire per terminal, copper column, from the on-disk 2017 scan): 1-1/2 in for 6–4 AWG, 2 in for 2–1 AWG, 2-1/2 in for 1/0, 3 in for 2/0, 3-1/2 in for 3/0–4/0, 4 in for 250–300 kcmil, and up to 8 in for 600–1000 kcmil. (The on-disk 2023 CSV carries the Table 312.6(A) header but not its rows, so the page asserts the 2017 values and does not claim a 2017→2023 row identity for this table — stated boundary in the method notes.) Conductors 4 AWG or larger entering/leaving through the opposite wall follow 300.4(G) instead (312.6(C)).
  • Pigtail (box-fill) space — Table 314.16(B) in 2017, Table 314.16(B)(1) in 2023. Where the connection is a pigtail — the wire loops and terminates with a connector rather than bending directly — the enclosure must have enough volume for the loops. 450.13 — Accessibility

The default is strong: all transformers and transformer vaults shall be readily accessible to qualified personnel for inspection and maintenance. Two carve-outs, both dry-type, both 1000 V nominal or less:

  • (A) Open Installations. On walls, columns, or structures in the open — not readily accessible is fine. (The transformer is out in the open; nobody is locking it away.)
  • (B) Hollow Space Installations. In hollow spaces of buildings not permanently closed in by structure — not exceeding 50 kVA — provided they meet 450.9 (ventilation) and 450.21(A) (separation from combustible materials). The 50 kVA number is the boundary: at 50 kVA you may hide it in a hollow space without ready access; at 51 kVA it must be readily accessible (or in a proper transformer room / vault per Part II / Part III).

450.14 — Disconnecting Means

Every transformer that is not a Class 2 (≤ 30 V, ≤ 100 VA) or Class 3 (≤ 60 V, ≤ 100 VA, Article 725) transformer needs a disconnecting means, located either:

  • In sight of the transformer (the common case — the disconnect on the same structure, within sight without climbing), or
  • In a remote location — but then it must be lockable in accordance with 110.25 (2017) / lockable open (2023 — the 2023 tightening: the device must lock in the open position, so a remote pad-mount disconnect that only locks in the closed position fails the 2023 text), and its location must be field marked on the transformer.

The Class 2/3 exemption is the one that trips people up: a 50 VA / 24 V power-limited transformer is exempt from 450.14 entirely (the ≤ 30 V / ≤ 100 VA limits make it power-limited), while a 50 kVA 480/208 distribution transformer is not — it needs the in-sight or remote-lockable-open disconnect and the field marking.

Net effect for an installation job. If you are placing a transformer, the requirements you satisfy are the same whether the jurisdiction is on 2017 or 2023: the guarding, the ventilation, the terminal-bar grounding, the eight nameplate items, the wiring space, the 50 kVA accessibility boundary, the in-sight-or-remote disconnect. The edition history matters for details — the IEEE standard years you cite in 450.9 notes, the no-storage marking on horizontal tops (2023 only), the 450.10 title, the 314.16(B)(1) reference you write up, and the 2023 requirement that a remote disconnect actually lock open.
