NEC 230.70 + 230.71 + 230.72 + 230.79 + 230.80 — Service Disconnecting Means

You ran the 220.82 service load and got a number — say 87.5 A or 105.79 A. What rating does the service disconnect itself get? That is Article 230, Part VI: 230.79 says the rating must be at least the calculated load, floored at 15 / 30 / 100 / 60 A by (A)–(D); 230.71 says one service, one disconnect (with the 2020 "two to six" exception); 230.72 says group them and mark them; 230.80 says the ratings must sum to the 230.79 value when more than one disconnect is used.

The question this article answers

Every service-load article on this site ends the same way: you have a calculated service current (A) and a recommended breaker (240.6(A) standard size). But the service disconnecting means — the main breaker, the main lugs, the fused disconnect — is rated by its own rule, 230.79, and that rule has a floor the load calc never mentions: a one-family dwelling is never served by less than a 100 A disconnect, even if the math says 40 A. And if the service is split across two to six disconnects, 230.80 adds a second check the load calc doesn't do — the ratings must sum to at least the 230.79 value.

These five sections are the service-side twin of the 220.82 card's 100 A one-family floor (the card's conductor pick is already floored at 100 A per 230.42(B) → 230.79(C) — the same number this article walks). Section titles verified identical across 2014 / 2017 / 2020 / 2023 on up.codes (NFPA 70): "Rating of Service Disconnecting Means" (230.79), "Combined Rating of Disconnects" (230.80), "Grouping of Disconnects" (230.72), "Maximum Number of Disconnects" (230.71).

230.79 — Rating of Service Disconnecting Means (the headline)

230.79 Rating of Service Disconnecting Means. The service disconnecting means shall have a rating not less than the calcu- lated load to be carried, determined in accordance with Part III, IV, or V of Article 220, as applicable. In no case shall the rating be lower than specified in 230.79(A), (B), (C), or (D). (A) One-Circuit Installations. For installations to supply only limited loads of a single branch circuit, the service disconnect- ing means shall have a rating of not less than 15 amperes. (B) Two-Circuit Installations. For installations consisting of not more than two 2-wire branch circuits, the service discon- necting means shall have a rating of not less than 30 amperes. (C) One-Family Dwellings. For a one-family dwelling, the serv- ice disconnecting means shall have a rating of not less than 100 amperes, 3-wire. (D) All Others. For all other installations, the service discon- necting means shall have a rating of not less than 60 amperes. Verbatim NEC 2017, official NFPA text (nec2017_full.txt lines 15220–15246). OCR line-wrap reflow normalized; two OCR hyphen artifacts corrected ("calcu-lated" → "calculated", "disconnect-ing" → "disconnecting", "discon-necting" → "disconnecting", "serv-ice" → "service"). No wording altered. Word-level diff against the verbatim 2020 text: identical (161/161 content words; only OCR-token differences). Note: the 2020 full-code scan prints "Part III, IV, or I of Article 220" — an OCR misread of "V" (Article 220 Part V is Farm Load Calculations in the 2017–2023 NECs; the 2017 official text on disk reads "Part III, IV, or V").
Read it in two halves. Lead-in: rating ≥ the calculated load (Article 220 — Part III standard, Part IV optional, Part V farm). (A)–(D): a floor "in no case" below it. The floors only ever raise the rating — they never shrink a larger calc. That is why the 220.82 card's recommended breaker can be 100 A on a light house (floor governs) and 110 A on a bigger one (calc governs). The four floors are exactly the four worked-example regimes on this page: 15 A (one circuit), 30 A (two circuits), 100 A (one-family), 60 A (everything else).

230.70 — General: location, marking, suitability

230.70 General. Means shall be provided to disconnect all conductors in a building or other structure from the service- entrance conductors. (A) Location. The service disconnecting means shall be instal- led in accordance with 230.70(A)(1), (A)(2), and (A)(3). (1) Readily Accessible Location. The service disconnecting means shall be installed at a readily accessible location either outside of a building or structure or inside nearest the point of entrance of the service conductors. (2) Bathrooms. Service disconnecting means shall not be installed in bathrooms. (3) Remote Control. Where a remote control device(s) is used to actuate the service disconnecting means, the service disconnecting means shall be located in accordance with 230.70(A)(1). (B) Marking. Each service disconnect shall be permanently marked to identify it as a service disconnect. (C) Suitable for Use. Each service disconnecting means shall be suitable for the prevailing conditions. Service equipment installed in hazardous (classified) locations shall comply with the requirements of Articles 500 through 517. Verbatim NEC 2017, official NFPA text (nec2017_full.txt lines 15058–15098). OCR line-wrap reflow normalized; page-header artifact ("2017 Edition NATIONAL ELECTRICAL CODE 230.72") removed from the middle of (A); two OCR hyphen artifacts corrected. No wording altered. The 2020 text's (C) clause is word-identical to the 2017 (verified against the verbatim 2020 full-code scan); the 2020 scan excerpt on disk begins mid-section at (C), so the (A)/(B) clauses are anchored to the 2017 text with the 2014–2023 title stability noted above.

230.70 sets the table: one disconnecting means for all conductors (230.74 covers simultaneous opening), at a readily accessible location, never in a bathroom, and permanently marked as a service disconnect. The rest of Part VI is detail on that one rule.

230.71 — Maximum Number of Disconnects (the 2020 restructure)

2017 wording (the "six in one place" rule)

230.71 Maximum Number of Disconnects. (A) General. The service disconnecting means for each serv- ice permitted by 230.2, or for each set of service-entrance conductors permitted by 230.40, Exception No. 1, 3, 4, or 5, shall consist of not more than six switches or sets of circuit breakers, or a combination of not more than six switches and sets of circuit breakers, mounted in a single enclosure, in a group of separate enclosures, or in or on a switchboard or in switchgear. There shall be not more than six sets of disconnects per service grouped in any one location. For the purpose of this section, disconnecting means instal- led as part of listed equipment and used solely for the following shall not be considered a service disconnecting means: (1) Power monitoring equipment (2) Surge-protective device(s) (3) Control circuit of the ground-fault protection system (4) Power-operable service disconnecting means (B) Single-Pole Units. Two or three single-pole switches or breakers, capable of individual operation, shall be permitted on multiwire circuits, one pole for each ungrounded conduc- tor, as one multipole disconnect, provided they are equipped with identified handle ties or a master handle to disconnect all conductors of the service with no more than six operations of the hand. Informational Note: See 408.36, Exception No. 1 and Exception No. 3, for service equipment in certain panelboards, and see 430.95 for service equipment in motor control centers. Verbatim NEC 2017, official NFPA text (nec2017_full.txt lines 15099–15140). OCR line-wrap reflow normalized; three OCR hyphen artifacts corrected ("serv-ice" → "service", "instal-led" → "installed", "conduc-tor" → "conductor"). No wording altered.

2020 wording (one disconnect unless 230.71(B))

230.71 Maximum Number of Disconnects. Each service shall have only one disconnecting means unless the requirements of 230.71(B) are met. (A) General. For the purpose of this section, disconnecting means installed as part of listed equipment and used solely for the following shall not be considered a service disconnecting means: (1) Power monitoring equipment (2) Surge-protective device(s) (3) Control circuit of the ground-fault protection system (4) Power-operable service disconnecting means (B) Two to Six Service Disconnecting Means. Two to six service disconnects shall be permitted for each service permitted by 230.2 or for each set of service-entrance conductors permitted by 230.40, Exception No. 1, 3, 4, or 5. The two to six service disconnecting means shall be permitted to consist of a combination of any of the following: (1) Separate enclosures with a main service disconnecting means in each enclosure (2) Panelboards with a main service disconnecting means in each panelboard enclosure (3) Switchboard(s) where there is only one service disconnect in each separate vertical section where there are barriers separating each vertical section (4) Service disconnects in switchgear or metering centers where each disconnect is located in a separate compartment Verbatim NEC 2020 full-code text (slideshare_nec2020.txt). OCR artifacts corrected and disclosed: "se1vice" → "service" (×5), "Exception No. l" → "Exception No. 1", "(I)" → "(1)"; the scan clumped the 230.71(A) list numbers ahead of the item text (two-column OCR artifact), so this quote renders the official (1)–(4) interleaving, and the 2020 230.71(B) list items are rendered in scan order. The four informational-note garbles omitted from this quote (they are notes, not code text; Note No. 1 names UL 67 for metering centers, Note No. 2 lists motor control centers / fused disconnects / circuit-breaker enclosures / transfer switches as (B)(1) examples). The 2017 (B) "Single-Pole Units" provision is not in 2020's 230.71 — it persists in the 225.33(B) and 230.90(A) language (both on disk).
The 2020 delta, precisely. Three things moved:
  1. Default flipped. 2017's (A) started "shall consist of not more than six…". 2020's lead-in starts "Each service shall have only one disconnecting means unless the requirements of 230.71(B) are met." — the multi-disconnect case is now the exception, not the rule.
  2. The "two to six" list is new. 230.71(B) (2020) enumerates the four permitted multi-disconnect configurations: separate enclosures each with its own main (1); panelboards each with a main (2); switchboards with one disconnect per barrier-separated vertical section (3); switchgear / metering centers with each disconnect in a separate compartment (4). The six-count cap remains ("two to six").
  3. Single-Pole Units left the section. The 2017 (B) (two or three single-pole breakers with handle ties / master handle = one multipole disconnect, six operations of the hand) is not in the 2020 230.71. Its verbatim twin (same "Two or three single-pole switches or breakers … identified handle ties or a master handle" text) now sits in 225.33(B) (disconnects for supplies, verbatim on disk), and 230.90(A) still references the grouping rule for overcurrent counting: "Single-pole circuit breakers, grouped in accordance with 230.71(B), shall be considered as one protective device." — both verbatim on disk.
The equipment-exclusion list (power monitoring, SPDs, GFCI control circuit, power-operable disconnects) moved from the 2017 (A) body to the 2020 (A) as a stand-alone "for the purpose of this section" rule — same four items, same meaning.

230.72 — Grouping of Disconnects

230.72 Grouping of Disconnects. (A) General. The two to six disconnects as permitted in 230.71 shall be grouped. Each disconnect shall be marked to indicate the load served. Exception: One of the two to six service disconnecting means permitted in 230.71, where used only for a water pump also intended to provide fire protection, shall be permitted to be located remote from the other disconnecting means. If remotely installed in accordance with this exception, a plaque shall be posted at the location of the remaining grouped disconnects denoting its location. (B) Additional Service Disconnecting Means. The one or more additional service disconnecting means for fire pumps, emergency systems, legally required standby, or optional standby services permitted by 230.2 shall be installed remote from the one to six service disconnecting means for normal service to minimize the possibility of simultaneous interruption of supply. (C) Access to Occupants. In a multiple-occupancy building, each occupant shall have access to the occupant's service disconnecting means. Exception: In a multiple-occupancy building where electric service and electrical maintenance are provided by the building management and where these are under continuous building management supervision, the service disconnecting means supplying more than one occupancy shall be permitted to be accessible to authorized management personnel only. Verbatim NEC 2017, official NFPA text (nec2017_full.txt lines 15140–15190). OCR line-wrap reflow normalized; page-footer artifacts ("70-89 230.72") removed. No wording altered. Word-level diff against the verbatim 2020 text: identical (215/219 content words; all 8 differing tokens are 2020-scan OCR misreads: "acwrdance"→"accordance", "al5o"→"also", "pennitted"→"permitted", "med"→"used", "pmvide"→"provide", "muuipleoccupancy"→"multiple-occupancy", page number 70-89→70-94). The 2020 text says "the two to six disconnects, if permitted in 230.71" — a wording echo of the new 230.71, same requirement.

Grouped (230.72(A)) means you can't scatter the two-to-six disconnects across the building — they live in one location, and each one is marked for the load it serves. The one escape hatch is the fire-protection water pump (remote location + plaque at the grouped spot). (B) is the fire-pump / emergency / standby rule: those extra disconnects go remote from the normal service disconnects on purpose, so a routine main-off doesn't kill the fire pump.

230.80 — Combined Rating of Disconnects (the sum test)

230.80 Combined Rating of Disconnects. Where the service disconnecting means consists of more than one switch or circuit breaker, as permitted by 230.71, the combined ratings of all the switches or circuit breakers used shall not be less than the rating required by 230.79. Verbatim NEC 2017, official NFPA text (nec2017_full.txt lines 15247–15251). OCR line-wrap reflow normalized; one OCR hyphen artifact corrected ("discon-necting" → "disconnecting"). No wording altered. Word-level diff against the verbatim 2020 text: identical (the only differing tokens are the 2017 page footer "70-90 ARTICLE 230 — SERVICES").

Two sentences, and they do real work on every multi-disconnect service: the sum of the individual ratings (not the largest one, not the parallel-combined capacity) must be at least the 230.79 rating of the service as a whole. The 230.79(A)–(D) floors still apply to the service as a single entity — a split one-family service still owes a 100 A floor — and then 230.80 checks the arithmetic of the split. EX6 below walks a 150 A service: 70+70 = 140 A fails, 100+70 = 170 A passes.

Two traps in one example. Trap 1: people check the largest disconnect (70 A) and call it a day — 230.80 checks the sum. Trap 2: the 230.79(A)–(D) floors are not waived by splitting — a one-family service split across two enclosures is still a one-family service and still owes the 100 A floor (230.80's "rating required by 230.79" is the whole-service rating, not 100 A per half).

Where 230.70–230.80 fit

  • Service-line conductor sizing: 230.42 + Table 310.16 — the Table 310.16 ampacity article → and the 215.2 feeder ampacity article → (the 230.79(C) floor appears in the tool's dwMinA constant).
