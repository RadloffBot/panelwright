Frequently asked questions

Q: What does Article 406 actually cover?
The devices, not the circuits: receptacles, cord connectors, and attachment plugs (cord caps) - their rating (406.3: not less than 15 A, 125 V or 15 A, 250 V; the 2023-new 406.3(D) termination rules), installation (406.4: grounding type, the (D)(1)-(D)(8) replacement rules), mounting (406.5: boxes, faceplates, the (J) 300 V adjacent-device limit), faceplates (406.6: thickness, grounding, the 406.6(D) 1 W night-light/USB limit), attachment plugs (406.7), noninterchangeability (406.8), damp/wet locations (406.9), grounding-type details (406.10-406.11), tamper-resistant receptacles (406.12), and - new in 2023 - single-pole separable connectors (406.13). Branch-circuit rules live in Article 210 (210.52 outlet counts, 210.8 GFCI, 210.12 AFCI) - this article is what makes the device at the end of each circuit legal.
Q: What changed in Article 406 from 2017 to 2023?
21 machine-diffed deltas (table above). The big ones: 406.2 became the reconditioned-equipment ban (definitions deleted); NEW 406.3(D) terminations (the 15 A / 14 AWG push-in corner + the CO/ALR copper-clad rule); the 406.3(E)→(F) controlled-marking renumber; 406.4(A) citation split; 406.4(C) re-anchored to 250.146; 406.4(D)(3) GFCI-listed sentence; 406.4(D)(4) extended to 210.12(C); the aluminum/CO/ALR replacement exception (D10); NEW (D)(7) automatically controlled replacements, (D)(8) GFPE, (G) floor receptacles (food-court GFCI); the 406.5(G)(2) below-sink face-up ban; the 406.6(D) 1 W faceplate limit (with the 2026 steel-screw Exception); 406.9(A) 90-degree hinged covers; the 406.9(C) bathtub zone (900 mm × 2.5 m + 4 exceptions); 406.10(C) reword; the 406.12 expansion (7 → 10 locations); and the brand-new 406.13 single-pole separable-connector section. No existing section number was deleted - the movement is additive, plus the 406.2 subject swap.
Q: Where are tamper-resistant receptacles required in 2023?
At every 15/20 A, 125/250 V nonlocking receptacle outlet in ten location classes: (1) all dwelling units (including boathouses, mobile/manufactured homes, garages, accessory buildings, common areas), (2) hotel/motel guest rooms and common areas, (3) child care facilities, (4) preschools and education facilities, (5) clinics/medical/dental/outpatient spaces (public offices, lobbies, waiting spaces), (6) places of awaiting transportation, gymnasiums, skating rinks, fitness centers, auditoriums, (7) dormitory units, (8) residential care/assisted living, social and substance abuse rehabilitation, group homes, (9) foster care facilities, nursing homes, psychiatric hospitals, (10) agricultural-building areas accessible to the public + common areas. Exceptions: over 1.7 m (5½ ft), luminaires/appliances, the appliance-space receptacles (single or duplex, not readily accessible), and nongrounding replacement receptacles per 406.4(D)(2)(a).
Q: What is the new 406.13, and what does it require?
Single-pole separable connectors (the "quick-disconnect" plugs) must be listed and labeled, locking or latching type, marked with the manufacturer and voltage/ampere ratings. The grounded-conductor side gets a white housing, the grounding-conductor side green. Ac/dc interchangeability requires listing + marking. And - the core of the section - use must be by a qualified person, with either source-interlocked supply connectors (no connect/disconnect while energized) or listed sequential-interlocking type connecting in the order EGC → grounded conductor → ungrounded (reverse for disconnect), with an 110.21(B) caution notice adjacent to the line connectors. The informational note points to ANSI-UL 1691-2014.
